# p2party — Related Work & Prior-Art / Novelty Assessment

*Honest source material for an applied/systems cryptography paper. Not marketing. Its job is to decide what the paper may legitimately claim, cite the closest prior art for each claim, and concede where a claim is already done.*

*This is the **unified** memo: the original topical prior-art pass merged with a venue-targeted supplement whose flagship-venue hits were confirmed venue+year against DBLP and IACR ePrint. Where a work appeared in both passes it is stated once, with the venue-confirmed attribution. See the note at the head of §6.*

---

## 1. Framing

p2party is an **applied/systems** contribution, not a primitive-level novelty. Nothing here invents a new cryptographic primitive, a new PAKE, a new KEM, or a new ratchet security notion — those are settled literature and the paper explicitly does **not** claim the Double Ratchet mechanism as novel. What p2party offers is a **specific engineering synthesis**: a browser-native, permissionless, WebRTC/DTLS peer-to-peer mesh whose transport is deliberately shaped to frustrate a passive traffic-analysis adversary (uniform 64 KiB frames, random in-chunk offsets, per-chunk random padding, pure-noise decoy chunks, shuffled/flooded multi-peer delivery, Merkle-authenticated reassembly, uniform-size read-receipts), carrying a Signal-style Double Ratchet seeded by a CPace PAKE that binds the transport's own DTLS certificate fingerprints and long-term Ed25519 identity keys.

Almost every *individual ingredient* has decades of precedent. The defensible contribution is therefore the **combination and its realization on browser transport**, plus one genuinely under-explored insight (C3) that survives the prior-art sweep. The paper's job is to scope its claims down to what the literature does *not* already cover and to cite the ancestors of everything it does not itself invent. This document does exactly that, and is deliberately unkind to the claims where reviewers will be.

Two figure-precision fixes that the paper must make regardless of framing:

- **The "~42-byte SPQR chunk" figure is wrong** and must be corrected. Source-verified: SPQR's shipped constant is `CHUNK_SIZE = 32` bytes (`src/encoding/polynomial.rs`); Signal's production per-message post-quantum PCS *budget* is **40 bytes/message** (Auerbach et al., NIST PQC 2025); the Signal blog's illustrative walkthrough uses **100-byte** chunks pedagogically; the PQShield secondary source says "42-byte" but is **not authoritative and must not be trusted over the 32-byte source constant**. This whole document is internally consistent on **32** (not 42). SPQR erasure-codes ML-KEM-768's encapsulation key (1184 B → ~37 chunks) and ciphertext (1088 B → ~34 chunks) into 32-byte chunks; the fragmentation family is **erasure codes** (Signal's own wording) — **not** "Reed–Solomon," which no primary source attributes to SPQR.
- **ML-KEM-768 EK+CT = 2272 bytes = ~71× the 32-byte classical DH message** — verified word-for-word in the Auerbach et al. primary source. This is the correct number to anchor the C3 contrast. The standardized hybrid **X-Wing ciphertext is a fixed 1120 bytes ≈ 1.71% of a single 64 KiB chunk** (X-Wing, IACR CiC 2024) — the quantitative anchor for the "fits free" half of C3.

## 2. Related Work, by dimension

*Type is marked `[R]` research, `[I]` in-the-wild implementation, `[S]` standard/draft.*

### A. Traffic-analysis-resistant transports, cover traffic & padding (targets C1/C2)

**Research.**
- **Chaffing and Winnowing** (Rivest, 1998) `[R]` — the conceptual ancestor of C2: a receiver strips MAC-verified "wheat" from indistinguishable "chaff"; an eavesdropper without the key must consider everything. This is *the* precedent for "confidentiality/effort via authentication, not hiding content." Note it is unencrypted-packet-level and explicitly permits *unshuffled* chaff; it has no notion of uniform framing, traffic-analysis, or a networked transport.
- **Tarzan** (Freedman & Morris, CCS 2002) `[R]` — closest P2P architectural ancestor: always-on, fixed-size, encrypted "mimic" cover-traffic links between peers, indistinguishable from real payload, with real data opportunistically substituted. Link-level, not per-message chunk-level; per-hop MAC not Merkle; single-path source-routed, not shuffle-and-flood.
- **Sphinx: A Compact and Provably Secure Mix Format** (Danezis & Goldberg, IEEE S&P 2009) `[R]` — the flagship-venue statement that a **bitwise- and length-uniform packet defeats a traffic observer**, and it itself traces the uniform-packaging idea to Chaum 1981. A **novelty threat to C1's general principle** (uniformity as a defense is old). But Sphinx is store-and-forward mix-relay, all-or-nothing (LIONESS/SPRP whole-packet cipher, no partial verification), and explicitly excludes decoy/economic defenses — the last of which *supports* the C2 contrast. Cite up front as the general-principle ancestor.
- **PURBs / Padmé** (Nikitin et al., PoPETs 2019) `[R]` — the nearest academic "uniform ciphertext" concept, but for a **single static blob** (padding to a size lattice; length leakage → O(log log M) bits). The paper *explicitly disclaims* the time-distribution-of-multiple-objects problem. No chunking, decoys, flood, or Merkle-auth. A **novelty threat to C1's ciphertext-shape principle** — a PoPETs reviewer's first citation.
- **A Systematic Approach to Developing and Evaluating Website Fingerprinting Defenses (Tamaraw)** (Cai, Nithyanand, Wang, Johnson, Goldberg, ACM CCS 2014) `[R]` — the canonical **constant-rate, padded-to-uniform** WF defense, and **C1's single sharpest prior-art threat**: a CCS/NDSS traffic-analysis reviewer opens with it. It is single-Tor-circuit, packet-granularity, with no decoy chunks, no shuffle-across-peers, no Merkle partial verification, and a censorship/DPI (not DTLS-mesh) threat model. Relates head-on; does not preempt the composition.
- **Maybenot: A Framework for Traffic Analysis Defenses** (Pulls & Witwer, WPES @ CCS 2023) `[R]` — a generic framework for **scheduling padding**. A novelty threat only if C1 is framed as "a padding framework"; it is not — distinguish explicitly.
- **DeTorrent: An Adversarial Padding-only Traffic Analysis Defense** (Holland, Carpenter, Oh, Hopper, PoPETs 2024) `[R]` and **RegulaTor: A Straightforward Website Fingerprinting Defense** (Holland & Hopper, PoPETs 2022) `[R]` — padding-only WF defenses are a **crowded, current line** (RegulaTor→DeTorrent lineage). C1 must not be sold as "another padding defense."
- **Deep Fingerprinting** (Sirinam, Imani, Juarez, Wright, ACM CCS 2018) `[R]` and **Automated Website Fingerprinting through Deep Learning** (Rimmer, Preuveneers, Juarez, van Goethem, Joosen, NDSS 2018) `[R]` — the **deep-learning attack baselines** any C1 evaluation must beat; not competing defenses. The measurement section must report against these, not just count/size/timing heuristics.
- **ScrambleSuit** (Winter, Pulls, Fuss, WPES 2013) `[R]`, **Walkie-Talkie** (Wang & Goldberg, USENIX Sec 2017) `[R]`, **FRONT** (Gong & Wang, USENIX Sec 2020) `[R]`, **FTE** (Dyer, Coull, Ristenpart, Shrimpton, CCS 2013) `[R]`, **Telex / decoy routing** (Wustrow et al., USENIX Sec 2011) `[R]` — the WF-defense / obfsproxy / decoy-routing family. All explore "pad + decoy + shape-morph to defeat count/size/timing" but at packet or page-load granularity over a **single Tor circuit / 1:1 tunnel**, for a censorship/DPI threat model, not a multi-peer E2E WebRTC mesh. Telex's "decoy" is a decoy *destination*, a different word entirely — worth one clarifying citation so the paper does not conflate the two "decoy" literatures.
- **Traffic Morphing** (Wright, Coull, Monrose, NDSS 2009) `[R]` — the canonical **opposite** economic philosophy to C2: reshape one traffic class to look like another at *minimal* overhead. The paper must engage this to justify choosing the expensive-for-both regime.

**In-the-wild / standards.**
- **obfs4** (Yawning Angel / Tor Project, 2014–) `[I]` — deployed at scale; makes every byte statistically indistinguishable from random (Elligator2 + SipHash-masked lengths). "Looks random" via masking of a single client-bridge stream, not uniform padded decoy chunks + Merkle + flood.

### B. Metadata-private / anonymity messaging systems & the economic-cost lineage (targets C1/C2)

**Research (mostly server-mediated mix / DP-noise lineage).**
- **Loopix** (Piotrowska et al., USENIX Sec 2017) `[R]` — the canonical cover-traffic-defeats-a-global-timing-adversary system (Poisson mix + loop/drop cover, Sphinx fixed-size packets). Closest "indistinguishable junk" precedent for C2's *intuition*, but framed as statistical/information-theoretic indistinguishability, **and it taxes the honest sender's bandwidth — the inverse of C2's cost model**; dedicated mix-server infrastructure, not a browser P2P link.
- **Vuvuzela** (van den Hooff et al., SOSP 2015) `[R]`, **Stadium** (Tyagi et al., SOSP 2017) `[R]`, **Karaoke** (Lazar, Gilad, Zeldovich, OSDI 2018) `[R]` — the differential-privacy dead-drop-noise family. Fixed-size (256-byte) messages + injected noise + mix-shuffle, but justified by formal (ε,δ)-DP bounds on server-observable metadata, requiring a trusted anytrust/honest-majority server federation. Wrong layer (inter-server links, not DTLS records) and wrong trust model (p2party trusts *no* relay).
- **XRD** (Kwon, Lu, Devadas, NSDI 2020) `[R]` — cryptographic (not DP) privacy via parallel mixnets; explicitly aims to *avoid* wasted bandwidth — again the opposite of C2.
- **Riposte** (Corrigan-Gibbs, Boneh, Mazières, IEEE S&P 2015) `[R]` — metadata-private write-only messaging via a PIR/DC-net "private information storage," scaling to millions of users. Different trust model (server-set, reverse-PIR), but a flagship-venue "impose global-observation cost" neighbor worth situating against.
- **Pung** (Angel & Setty, OSDI 2016) `[R]` — titled *Unobservable Communication over **Fully Untrusted Infrastructure***: the one system that makes **fully untrusted infrastructure** do genuinely expensive work (computational PIR over the whole DB), the closest "impose cost" precedent. But the cost lands on the untrusted PIR **server's CPU**, not on a passive DTLS eavesdropper's storage.
- **Dissent** (Corrigan-Gibbs & Ford, CCS 2010) `[R]` and **Herbivore** (Goel et al., Cornell TR 2003) `[R]` — DC-net-derived P2P ancestors where members transmit every round regardless of real data. Genuine "waste-by-construction" pedigree — but both **explicitly optimize *away* wasted bits** (Dissent: "none of these bits are wasted padding"; Herbivore reserves slots only for actual senders and criticizes constant-rate P5 for wasting bandwidth). They are the *contrast* to C2, not its precedent.
- **Pricing via Processing or Combatting Junk Mail** (Dwork & Naor, CRYPTO 1992) `[R]` — the **origin of the cost-asymmetric defense** and the root C2's "offensive cryptography" framing should own. It **inverts every concrete parameter of C2**: the cost falls on the message *sender* (to earn acceptance), taxes *CPU per message via a solved puzzle*, and is an application-layer anti-abuse gate. C2 taxes a *passive observer* on *storage + downstream cryptanalytic effort*, and no one solves a puzzle. The novelty is the *direction* of the asymmetry, not the abstraction.
- **Client Puzzles: A Cryptographic Countermeasure Against Connection Depletion** (Juels & Brainard, NDSS 1999) `[R]` — the same "impose asymmetric cost on the adversary" principle applied to DoS (compute, not storage). Historical root; not a competing mechanism.
- **EMC2: Johnny Should Use Secret Sharing** (Chandran, Demuth, Edalatnejad, Linsner, Reuter, Schneider, WPES @ CCS 2024) `[R]` — the closest *surveillance-cost* argument to C2: an adversary must observe **all** channels to reconstruct anything (n-of-n secret sharing across independent external channels). Distinguish precisely from C2's "must **store** all chunks within one mesh" (decoy/padding volume, not cross-channel sharing).
- **TEEMS: A TEE-based Metadata-protected Messaging System** (Sasy, Johnson, Goldberg, PoPETs 2025) `[R]` — a must-cite contrast for *why p2party avoids TEEs* and uses decoy-volume storage economics instead.
- **Alpenhorn** (Lazar & Zeldovich, OSDI 2016) `[R]` — metadata-private bootstrapping/friend-discovery via anytrust IBE servers; same goal as C5 but server-trusting, not a peer PAKE.
- **On the Economics of Anonymity** (Acquisti, Dingledine, Syverson, FC 2003) `[R]` — the field's canonical "economics" paper, cited precisely to show its "economics" means *participant-incentive game theory*, a different sense than C2's "impose cost on the eavesdropper."
- **Strong Anonymity for Mesh Messaging** (Perry, Spang, Eskandarian, Boneh, arXiv 2022) `[R]` — the literature's nearest "P2P mesh + metadata privacy" neighbor. But Bluetooth radio (not WebRTC), whole-message-granularity fixed-size cover traffic + anti-entropy gossip, classical ElGamal signcryption, no ratchet/PQ, QR/web-of-trust bootstrap (not PAKE).
- **SoK: Metadata-Protecting Communication Systems** (Sasy & Goldberg, PoPETs 2024) `[R]` — the **taxonomy any "we use cover traffic for metadata privacy" claim is measured against**, and a **novelty threat to C1/C2's framing**: a privacy reviewer will place p2party in this landscape and ask which cell is new (answer: browser-native, serverless P2P mesh + PQ ratchet — a cell the SoK does not cover).
- **The Last Hop Attack: Why Loop Cover Traffic over Fixed Cascades Threatens Anonymity** (Weisenseel, Döpmann, Tschorsch, PoPETs 2025) `[R]` — state-of-the-art cover-traffic **attack**; reviewers will ask whether the shuffle-and-flood mesh (not a fixed cascade) is vulnerable to this class. The evaluation must answer it.
- **Generic Anonymity Wrapper for Messaging Protocols** (Thiemt, Rösler, Bienstock, Schmidt, Dodis; ACM CCS 2025, "A Practical Wrapper Protocol for Metadata-Hiding," RWC 2026 talk) `[R]` — the closest current "anonymity-in-messaging" thread; it hides *identity/metadata* at a wrapper layer, complementary to (not competing with) C1/C2's traffic-pattern shaping.

**In-the-wild.**
- **Signal Sealed Sender** (Signal, 2018) `[I]` + **Improving Signal's Sealed Sender** (Martiny et al., NDSS 2021) `[R]` — hides only sender identity from the server; a statistical-disclosure attack on predictable delivery-receipt timing de-anonymizes senders. **Motivates rather than preempts C1**, and specifically validates p2party's design choice to make **reverse read-receipts uniform-size** — closing exactly the predictable-receipt loophole this attack exploited. (Correction to earlier framing: real Signal already bucket-pads to multiples of 160 bytes, and the NDSS'21 attack explicitly excludes group messaging and network metadata — do not overstate it.)

### C. Steganographic / covert-channel & decoy-slot techniques (targets C1 + the C3 decoy-rekey half)

**Research.**
- **Slitheen** (Bocovich & Goldberg, CCS 2016) `[R]` — the closest "only the keyholder can pick the real bytes out of what looks like ordinary cover traffic" technique: overwrites an innocuous decoy site's download with covert data, statistically identical in size/timing/count. Network/decoy-routing layer (imitating a specific overt destination), not a uniform-64 KiB P2P mesh. No PQ/rekey angle.
- **Slitheen++: Stealth TLS-based Decoy Routing** (Birtel & Rossow, USENIX FOCI 2020) `[R]` — "defeat a TLS/DTLS-record observer" at a different layer; a contrast citation for the record-observer threat model.
- **Decoy Routing** (Karlin et al., FOCI 2011) `[R]` — foundational "a connection carries a hidden signal invisible to the observer but actionable by a cooperating in-path party." ~15-year lineage (Telex/Cirripede/TapDance/Rebound/Slitheen) the paper should acknowledge when framing C1's decoy-detection as new. **Terminology-collision warning:** FOCI's "decoy routing" ≠ p2party's "decoy chunk" — preempt reviewer confusion.
- **Provably Secure Steganography** (Hopper, Langford, von Ahn, CRYPTO 2002) `[R]` — the closest **theory anchor** for decoy-chunk indistinguishability (general steganographic security, not chunk/DTLS-specific). Bears on C1 and the C3 decoy-slot half; cite as the formal backdrop against which "hide a rekey in a decoy slot" is defined. This is also why the C3 decoy-slot half must be framed as *uncrowded*, not *first-in-principle*.
- **Learning to Behave: Improving Covert Channel Security with Behavior-Based Designs** (Wails, Stange, Troper, Caliskan, Dingledine, Jansen, Sherr, PoPETs 2022) `[R]` — covert-channel hiding literature; p2party's uniform chunks sidestep the behavioral-realism problem it tackles. Contrast citation.
- **Deniable file systems** — StegFS (McDonald & Kuhn, IH 1999), Rubberhose, TrueCrypt hidden volumes `[R]` — same chaff/decoy-indistinguishability idea but **at rest** (disk blocks), a different problem (coercion-resistant storage). Does not preempt any of C1–C5.
- **On the Practical Feasibility of Harvest-Now, Decrypt-Later Attacks** (Blanco-Romero et al., arXiv:2603.01091, 2026) `[R]` — the critique the paper must pre-empt for C2: an explicit cost model arguing storage-inflation "penalizes both sides while quantum-cost inflation targets the adversary alone," recommending rekeying over storage-inflation defenses. **Different threat model** (HNDL future-decryption cost vs. p2party's real-time correlation/selection cost) — the paper must distinguish "wasting analysis effort *now*" from "inflating storage for *later*." **[citation to verify: arXiv id/authorship/quote unconfirmed — this preprint was not deep-read; confirm before publication]**

**In-the-wild.**
- **BitTorrent MSE/PE** (Azureus/Vuze community, 2006–) `[I]` — the closest deployed P2P precedent for padding/decoys: inserts obfuscating padding messages into a swarm to hide protocol structure. But **variable-size** (RC4-obfuscated), no Merkle, no uniform framing — and independent fingerprinting research identifies it at >96% accuracy from packet size/timing. A "prior attempt that failed for lack of uniformity" — it *strengthens* p2party's uniform-chunk rationale.

### D. Post-quantum ratchets, ratchet-security models & large-KEM-ciphertext transport (targets C3, with C4 model support)

**Research — PQ-ratchet transport (all *verify* the C3 contrast; none preempt it).**
- **Post-Quantum Ratcheting for Signal** (Auerbach, Dodis, Jost, Katsumata, Prest, Schmidt, NIST PQC 2025) `[R]` — full-text-verified primary source. ML-KEM EK+CT = 2272 B = "71 times longer" than 32-B DH; production caps PCS overhead at **40 B/message**; the Sparse-CKA contribution erasure-codes the big handshake into small chunks (tested 32/128/512 B) *because production bandwidth cannot absorb 2272 B at once*. **Zero mention** anywhere in 37 pages of cover traffic, decoys, or hiding rekeys from a metadata observer.
- **The Triple Ratchet: A Bandwidth Efficient Hybrid-Secure Signal Protocol** (Dodis, Jost, Katsumata, Prest, Schmidt, EUROCRYPT 2025, pp. 302–331) `[R]` — **the C3 anchor/comparator.** State-of-the-art PQ-ratchet transport fix on a *message-oriented* transport is *still* **erasure-coding across many small messages + a new smaller KEM ("Katana," 2272→1416 bytes)** — i.e. it solves the problem *the hard way* precisely because Signal's transport has no uniform-large-chunk cover primitive to exploit. States plainly a naive one-shot PQ send is "an unacceptable size increase," and that PQ3 does "many consecutive re-transmissions of the same (long!) Kyber public keys and ciphertexts." Confirms the entire research line exists to solve a bandwidth-amortization problem p2party sidesteps by construction.
- **How to Compare Bandwidth Constrained Two-Party Secure Messaging Protocols** (Auerbach, Dodis, Jost, Katsumata, Schmidt, USENIX Security 2025) `[R]` — the field treats **"bandwidth budget per message" as the central axis** — the very constraint C3 dissolves. Same-period, top-tier evidence the constraint is real.
- **A Formal Analysis of Apple's iMessage PQ3 Protocol** (Linker, Sasse, Basin, USENIX Security 2025) `[R]` — models KEM material sent in-band on the ratchet's schedule; threat model is confidentiality/authentication, not traffic analysis. Leaves the cover-chunk angle untouched.
- **Formal Verification of the PQXDH Protocol** (Bhargavan, Jacomme, Kiefer, Schmidt, USENIX Security 2024) `[R]` — Signal's deployed PQ *handshake* (not the ratchet), formally verified. Confidentiality/authentication scope; silent on transport economics. Anchors "PQXDH is a vetted handshake we can seed from."
- **Post-quantum Asynchronous Deniable Key Exchange and the Signal Handshake** (Brendel, Fiedler, Günther, Janson, Stebila, PKC 2022) `[R]` — the formal PQ X3DH analogue; **transport-agnostic** (never sizes or carries the ciphertext). (See §7 Gaps for the hedged "internal construction named SPQR" claim, which the paper must *not* assert as fact.)
- **A Deniability Analysis of Signal's Initial Handshake PQXDH** (Fiedler & Janson, PoPETs 2024) `[R]` — PQXDH deniability context; silent on transport/sizes. Background for the handshake-deniability discussion.

**Research — ratchet-security models (definitional baseline; target C4's "we are formalizably a Double Ratchet" and C3's PQ-soundness).**
- **The Double Ratchet: Security Notions, Proofs, and Modularization** (Alwen, Coretti, Dodis, EUROCRYPT 2019) `[R]` — the KEM-abstraction (CKA) antecedent; establishes the academic PQ-ratchet line focuses on the security-proof side, never transport-level concealment. Its generic CKA abstraction is exactly why instantiating a Double Ratchet with an ML-KEM/X-Wing KEM is provably sound — the paper should say "we instantiate ACD's CKA with X-Wing and inherit their composition theorem."
- **A Formal Security Analysis of the Signal Messaging Protocol** (Cohn-Gordon, Cremers, Dowling, Garratt, Stebila, IEEE **EuroS&P 2017**; extended **Journal of Cryptology 33(4), 2020**) `[R]` — the as-implemented security model. **Attribute precisely as EuroS&P / J. Cryptology — NOT Oakland IEEE S&P and NOT EUROCRYPT/CRYPTO.** Must-cite bedrock for C4's "we are formalizably a Double Ratchet and our PQ instantiation is sound," and it supplies vocabulary (FS, PCS, immediate decryption) the proof sketch should reuse; it cannot preempt a systems contribution.
- **Towards Bidirectional Ratcheted Key Exchange** (Poettering & Rösler, CRYPTO 2018) `[R]` and **Efficient Ratcheting: Almost-Optimal Guarantees for Secure Messaging** (Jost, Maurer, Mularczyk, EUROCRYPT 2019) `[R]` — stronger ratchet-security-model and efficiency/PCS comparison points, one abstraction layer above any implementation.

**In-the-wild / standards (contrast cases).**
- **SPQR** (Signal blog + `signalapp/SparsePostQuantumRatchet`, 2025) `[I]` — shreds ML-KEM-768 EK (1184 B → ~37 chunks) and CT (1088 B → ~34 chunks) into **32-byte erasure-coded** chunks (deliberately matched to the legacy ECDH ratchet's 32-byte-per-message overhead so the wire format doesn't change size) piggybacked on ordinary messages, precisely because the existing transport can't carry >1 KB cheaply and a size spike would enable a downgrade/censorship attack. The **mirror image** of C3. Does **not** hide chunks in decoys. (Signal's blog says only "erasure codes" — do **not** attribute "Reed–Solomon"; the exact code family is in the EUROCRYPT 2025 paper body and is unverified from primary text — see §7 Gaps.)
- **iMessage PQ3** (Apple, 2024) `[I]` — the opposite tradeoff: sends the full ~2 KB ciphertext directly and throttles rekey frequency (~every 50 messages / ≥ every 7 days, because a PQ-KEM rekey "increases message size by more than 2 kilobytes"); pads message length separately via Padmé. Never disguises the rekey. Real deployed evidence a big PQ ct is *not* free on a normal transport. (Deployment in Apple's official write-up and F. Jacobs, *Designing iMessage PQ3*, Real World Crypto 2024 invited talk; formally analyzed separately by Linker et al., USENIX Sec 2025.) Does not preempt C3.
- **PQShield "Diving into Signal's new PQ protocol"** (2025) `[I/blog]` — secondary source that states **"42-byte chunks"**; this figure is **untrustworthy** and must not be cited over the source-verified 32-byte constant.
- **FIPS 203 (ML-KEM)** (NIST, 2024) `[S]` — authoritative CT sizes 768/1088/1568 B — grounds the "fits in one 64 KiB chunk" arithmetic.
- **X-Wing: The Hybrid KEM** (Barbosa, Connolly, Duarte, Kaiser, Schwabe, Varner, Westerbaan) `[R]` — published in **IACR Communications in Cryptology 2024** (a **journal**, ePrint 2024/039), with a companion CFRG draft (`draft-connolly-cfrg-xwing-kem`). Hybrid X25519+ML-KEM-768 CT is a fixed **1120 B ≈ 1.71% of a 64 KiB chunk**, with no fragmentation logic; the spec assumes a transport that just carries it. Directly supports "the whole hybrid ciphertext fits in one p2party chunk."
- **Kemeleon: Elligator-like Obfuscation for Post-Quantum Cryptography** (Günther, Rosenberg, Stebila, Veitch, Real World Crypto 2025) `[R]` — the sharpest **content-level** objection to the C3 decoy-slot sub-claim: **raw ML-KEM public keys and ciphertexts are NOT computationally indistinguishable from uniform random bytes** (unlike Elligator-encoded X25519). A raw X-Wing/ML-KEM ct placed in a "pure-noise" decoy chunk is therefore **statistically fingerprintable** — an observer can tell which "decoys" are secretly real rekeys under a byte-level test. Kemeleon supplies the encoding that restores byte-uniformity. See C3 for the required honest response.

### E. In-the-wild browser / P2P E2E ratchet & transfer implementations (targets C4)

- **Matrix — Olm / Megolm / vodozemac / matrix-rust-sdk-crypto-wasm / matrix-js-sdk** (2016–2025) `[I]` — **production Double Ratchet in the browser** (Element Web) via WASM, with **IndexedDB** session persistence. Does *not* use non-extractable WebCrypto keys (keys live in WASM linear memory / optionally-encrypted IndexedDB blob) and requires a homeserver account (not permissionless URL).
- **Wire — Proteus / @wireapp/core** (2016–2024) `[I]` — genuine Double Ratchet in-browser (libsodium WASM), IndexedDB sessions. No non-extractable-WebCrypto storage; requires a Wire backend.
- **Wire — core-crypto / OpenMLS** (2022–2025) `[I]` — MLS (RFC 9420) compiled to WASM with "encrypted persistent storage"; its own docs admit the WASM encryption-at-rest "isn't validated nor audited."
- **Wrongthink** (FlintSH, 2023–2024) `[I]` — **the single strongest C4 threat**: a browser app advertised literally as "peer-to-peer … end-to-end encryption (Double Ratchet over DTLS (WebRTC))", PeerJS signaling + in-house TweetNaCl ratchet. Niche/unaudited, PeerJS-ID (not a shared-URL mesh), no documented IndexedDB/non-extractable-key story, zero traffic-analysis defense.
- **2key-ratchet** (PeculiarVentures) `[I]` — Signal Double Ratchet + X3DH ported to **WebCrypto** in TypeScript, predating protocol-v3. Two-party, not WebRTC/mesh; no confirmed non-extractable-key/IndexedDB use; substitutes P-256 for X25519. Now archived, points to `pqc-ratchet`.
- **pqc-ratchet** (PeculiarVentures) `[I]` — a **PQ** Double Ratchet (ML-KEM-768+X25519, ML-DSA-65) browser-compatible via WebCrypto + `@noble/post-quantum`. Undercuts a naive "first PQ ratchet in a browser" framing. No transport/chunking, IndexedDB, mesh, or PAKE. "Not production-ready."
- **positive-intentions "P2P Signal Protocol"** (2023–2026) `[I]` — Signal Double Ratchet ported to P2P WebRTC in-browser, live X3DH handshake, server-less. IndexedDB persistence listed as *future* work; no non-extractable-key discussion; strictly two-party, online-only; no mesh/chunking.
- **ts-mls** (Luka Jacobowitz) `[I]` — TypeScript MLS (RFC 9420) with PQ/hybrid ciphersuites (ML-KEM, X-Wing), WebCrypto-capable. MLS not Double Ratchet; no documented IndexedDB/non-extractable-key persistence.
- **Magic Wormhole** (Warner; CLI + `wormhole.page` WASM) `[I]` — SPAKE2-from-codeword → NaCl secretbox file transfer. No ratchet, no chunk/decoy transport, no DTLS-fingerprint/identity binding. Establishes PAKE-over-P2P-transfer as commodity.
- **PairDrop / Snapdrop** (schlagmichdoch/RobinLinus) `[I]` and **FilePizza** (kern) `[I]` — shared-URL → WebRTC transfer whose confidentiality "is mandatory … completely done by the browser itself" (bare DTLS). **Negative precedent**: the deployed browser-transfer niche does *nothing* at the C1/C2/C3/C5 level — that space is open.
- **js-libp2p WebRTC** (Protocol Labs) `[I]` — real permissionless browser-to-browser WebRTC mesh, PeerId-authenticated, DTLS + Noise AKE. Noise is a per-session AKE, not a persistent ratchet; no PAKE/PIN, no chunk defense.

### F. Browser at-rest key protection & WebCrypto (targets C4)

- **Web Cryptography API** (W3C Rec 2017 / L2 draft 2025) `[S]` — defines the `CryptoKey` `extractable` slot; the **substrate** C4 builds on, not competing prior art.
- **Security Analysis of the W3C Web Cryptography API** (Cairns, Halpin, Steel, SSR 2016) `[R]` — formal (AVISPA/SAT) analysis; a key-wrapping attack on secret material was found and "remains open." The citation for honestly caveating non-extractability as *not* HSM-equivalent (an XSS attacker can't exfiltrate the key but *can invoke it* while injected).
- **WebCrypto API: Prevalence and Possible Developer Mistakes** (ACM DL, 2022; authorship unconfirmed) `[R]` — empirical study documenting real-world key-extractability mistakes; shows correct non-extractable usage is *not* the default, i.e. nontrivial engineering.
- **Storing Cryptographic Keys in Persistent Browser Storage** (Corella, Pomcor, 2017) `[R]` — **threatens the C4 mechanism narrowly**: explicitly proposes generating a non-extractable WebCrypto keypair and persisting the `CryptoKey` in IndexedDB as an at-rest scheme, discussing XSS/malware/physical-capture. Establishes "non-extractable CryptoKey persisted in IndexedDB" as a **known general pattern since 2017**.
- **secure-webstore** (AKASHAorg) `[I]` — encrypted IndexedDB via a passphrase-derived key held in JS memory (*not* a non-extractable CryptoKey). Negative data point: "secure browser storage" libraries commonly do *not* adopt the non-extractable pattern, so p2party's choice is deliberate, not boilerplate.
- **SoK: Secure Messaging** (Unger et al., IEEE S&P 2015) `[R]` — the expected reviewer baseline that **explicitly scopes out endpoint compromise** ("we assume that the endpoints … are secure"; "malware and hardware attacks are out of scope"). C4's contribution — non-extractable WebCrypto keys defeating JS-level key exfiltration after an origin compromise — sits *precisely in the gap this canonical SoK declares out of scope*. Frame C4 as a deliberate relaxation of that endpoint-security assumption. (Also the field taxonomy relevant to C1/C2/C5; see §G.)

### G. PAKE & channel binding to transport certs (targets C5)

**Standards / drafts.**
- **CPace** (draft-irtf-cfrg-cpace-21; Haase, Labrique) `[S]` — defines CI (channel identifier) and ADa/ADb associated-data fields to bind the PAKE to "networking addresses … or party identity strings," but leaves the integration entirely to the application and never discusses a TLS/DTLS certificate or fingerprint. Anticipates C5's move in the abstract; does not specify it.
- **RFC 9266 (TLS 1.3 channel bindings)** `[S]`, **RFC 5056 (channel bindings)** `[S]` — the generic "bind an outer auth protocol to the underlying secure channel" primitives (SASL/GSS-API/EAP over TLS). Never applied to DTLS/WebRTC, never fused with a PAKE, never fold in a separate identity key. (See §7 Gaps: the `tls-exporter`/`tls-unique` / RFC 5929 lineage is the likely nearest prior art to search next.)
- **RFC 8122 (SDP DTLS fingerprint)** `[S]` — the exact fingerprint-binding primitive p2party's PAKE input consumes; but provides **no defense when signaling itself is adversarial** (fingerprint is simply substituted). This is the vulnerability C5 closes, not a solution.
- **RFC 8827 (WebRTC Security Architecture / IdP)** `[S]` — **threatens C5's framing, not its mechanism**: WebRTC already binds a peer's asserted identity to the DTLS fingerprint to defeat a malicious signaling server ("can strip … but cannot forge" assertions). But it requires a **trusted federated Identity Provider**, and a malicious signaling server *can still strip* the assertion. The paper must cite this and argue the PAKE+PIN route removes the IdP trust anchor entirely.
- **RFC 6189 (ZRTP)** `[S]` — the classic human-verified-SAS + key-continuity/TOFU MITM defense. Runs its own DH in the SRTP *media* path, uses a spoken SAS (not a PAKE), never touches a DTLS certificate fingerprint or a persistent identity key.
- **RFC 9807 (OPAQUE)** `[S]` — asymmetric client-server aPAKE; different trust model than balanced peer-to-peer CPace; no transport/identity channel binding. Included for PAKE-landscape completeness.

**Research / implementations.**
- **Security Analysis of CPace** (Abdalla, Haase, Hesse, ASIACRYPT 2021, pp. 711–741) `[R]` — the **primitive's security pedigree**: a UC-secure-under-adaptive-corruptions proof of the exact **balanced** PAKE C5 builds on (both sides hold the PIN; no server-side verifier to steal; MapToPoint not idealized). But it takes a **generic, opaque channel/session identifier** input, treated as environment-supplied auxiliary data — the proof never specifies or evaluates a real-world instantiation of that slot. C5's novelty is precisely *what* fills it. Cite as the pedigree that justifies using CPace rather than rolling one's own.
- **Universally Composable Relaxed Password Authenticated Key Exchange** (Abdalla, Barbosa, Bradley, Jarecki, Katz, Xu, CRYPTO 2020) `[R]` — the lazy-extraction PAKE UC toolkit CPace's own analysis builds on; background for the security argument.
- **OPAQUE: An Asymmetric PAKE Secure Against Pre-Computation** (Jarecki, Krawczyk, Xu, EUROCRYPT 2018) `[R]` — the aPAKE counterpoint at the paper level (complementing RFC 9807); motivates why p2party picks a *balanced* PAKE.
- **Triple Handshakes and Cookie Cutters: Breaking and Fixing Authentication over TLS** (Bhargavan, Delignat-Lavaud, Fournet, Pironti, Strub, IEEE S&P 2014) `[R]` — establishes that **binding higher-layer auth to the transport session/certificate** is a known, respected technique for closing cross-protocol MITM gaps — so C5's *general principle* is established, not invented here. But it is a TLS attack/fix paper, not a PAKE-input-binding construction.
- **SoK: Secure Messaging** (Unger et al., IEEE S&P 2015) `[R]` — the canonical taxonomy of trust-establishment (PKI, key directories, TOFU, OOB/SAS, password-based). Situates C5's PAKE-based trust in the known design space; does not itself bind a PAKE to WebRTC fingerprints + identity key.
- **PakeMail** (Vazquez Sandoval, Atashpendar, Lenzini, Ryan, arXiv 2021) `[R]` — closest academic analog in spirit: PAKE to bootstrap authentication in a decentralized (no-PKI, no-TTP) messaging setting from a low-entropy secret. But targets email/XMPP identity, never binds a DTLS fingerprint, no data-channel or Ed25519-device component.
- **How to kickstart Secure Message Transfer with SAS and OOB** (Beskorovajnov & Müller-Quade, ePrint 2025/1598) `[R]` — very recent; critiques "PAKE one-code" designs (Magic-Wormhole-style) and argues SAS+OOB composed with KEM/DEM is preferable, decoupling rendezvous from authentication. Observes that consumer/WebRTC-style channels commonly lack a user-verifiable binding to the transport — exactly C5's gap — but its own fix is SAS/OOB, not a PAKE bound to DTLS fingerprints. Strong motivating/contrast citation.
- **Cryptographic Binding Should Not Be Optional (FIDO UAF)** (Golaszewski et al., SSR 2025) `[R]` — Dolev-Yao proof that optional/incomplete channel binding enables cross-session relay. General motivation for why C5's binding must be mandatory and cryptographically enforced; scoped to FIDO UAF, not PAKE/WebRTC.
- **Reclaiming our Passwords: Protecting E2EE from a Malicious Zoom Server** (Cremers, Ronen, Zhao, Real World Crypto 2024) `[R]` — "PAKE against a malicious coordination server" is an active real-world theme; not DTLS-fingerprint binding, but the closest deployed-motivation neighbor for C5's trust model.
- **webwormhole** (saljam, 2019–2024) `[I]` — **threatens C5**: already runs **CPace** (the same PAKE) over the WebRTC exchange and **binds both peers' DTLS certificate fingerprints** into the PAKE-protected session descriptions, in-browser. Preempts "CPace binding DTLS fingerprints" as a novel mechanism. Does **not** bind long-term Ed25519 identity keys and has **no persistent ratchet** (one-shot ephemeral PAKE per transfer).
- **Magic-Wormhole client-to-client protocol** (Warner et al.) `[I]` — balanced SPAKE2 with idA/idB identity strings folded into key-derivation + an optional manual "verifier." Shows PAKE-with-bound-identity-strings is established, but relays over a mailbox server via plain TCP — no certificate fingerprint to bind.

## 3. Per-claim novelty assessment

Verdict scale: **NOVEL** / **NOVEL-ADJACENT** (incremental over known work) / **ALREADY-DONE** (someone shipped or published it). **C3 is the only NOVEL claim; C1/C2/C4/C5 are all prior-art-contested and are conceded as such.**

### C1 — Traffic-analysis-resistant uniform-chunk-flood transport over browser WebRTC/DTLS
**Closest prior art:** Sphinx (S&P 2009, uniform packet defeats a traffic observer, traces to Chaum 1981); Tamaraw (CCS 2014, constant-rate padded-to-uniform — the sharpest single threat); PURBs (PoPETs 2019, uniform single blob); SoK: Metadata-Protecting Communication Systems (PoPETs 2024, the taxonomy); Maybenot (WPES 2023) / DeTorrent (PoPETs 2024) (padding frameworks / padding-only defenses are a crowded line); Tarzan (P2P indistinguishable cover traffic, link-level); Loopix (cover traffic vs. traffic analysis, sender-pays); Slitheen / decoy routing (only the keyholder extracts real bytes from what looks like cover); obfs4 & BitTorrent MSE (in-the-wild padded/random-looking transports); Karaoke/Vuvuzela (fixed-size + noise + shuffle, server mixnet); Strong Anonymity for Mesh Messaging (P2P + metadata privacy, whole-message granularity). Evaluation baselines to beat: Deep Fingerprinting (CCS 2018) and Rimmer (NDSS 2018); attack to survive: the Last Hop Attack class (PoPETs 2025).

**How p2party differs:** No prior system — research or deployed — combines *all* of: uniform fixed-size 64 KiB frames **+** real data at a random in-chunk offset **+** per-chunk random padding **+** dedicated pure-noise decoy chunks **+** shuffled/flooded multi-peer delivery **+** Merkle-authenticated chunk reassembly (enabling partial verification / resumable transfer, which Sphinx's all-or-nothing whole-packet SPRP cannot) **+** uniform-size reverse read-receipts, evaluated against a **DTLS-record observer or the signaling/TURN server on one link** rather than a store-and-forward mix relay or a Tor circuit. The analogues split cleanly by which subset they cover (PURBs = uniform blob, no flow; Sphinx/Tarzan/Loopix = link-level/mix cover; WF/obfs family = single-circuit; mesh-messaging = whole-message granularity). The *category* ("uniform / fixed-size, content-independent packaging defeats size/format traffic analysis") is decades old (Sphinx→Chaum 1981) and **must not be claimed**.

**Verdict: NOVEL-ADJACENT.** The bundle-as-a-whole and its browser/WebRTC realization appear unpublished and unshipped, but every ingredient has a named ancestor. Claim the *specific mechanism bundle + browser transport realization + DTLS-mesh threat model + the C3 tie-in*, explicitly disclaiming the underlying "uniformity as defense" principle (Sphinx/Tamaraw/PURBs) up front, and benchmarking against the DL attacks, not just count/size heuristics.

### C2 — "Offensive cryptography": economic/adversarial cost-imposition on a passive metadata observer
**Closest prior art:** Dwork–Naor "Pricing via Processing" (CRYPTO 1992, the origin of cost-asymmetric defense) and Client Puzzles (NDSS 1999) for the "impose asymmetric cost on an adversary" abstraction; Chaffing-and-winnowing (Rivest 1998) for the "attacker must sift indistinguishable chaff" mechanism; Pung (*fully untrusted infrastructure*, OSDI 2016) for the only system that makes untrusted infrastructure do provably expensive work (but on a *server's CPU*, not a passive eavesdropper); EMC2 (WPES 2024) for the closest "must observe ALL channels" surveillance-cost argument; TEEMS (PoPETs 2025) as the TEE contrast; Dissent/Herbivore for DC-net "everyone transmits" pedigree (which explicitly minimizes waste); On the Economics of Anonymity for the term "economics" (participant incentives, a different sense); Traffic Morphing as the explicit *opposite* philosophy; the HNDL-feasibility paper as the direct critique.

**How p2party differs:** p2party targets a **passive DTLS/network observer (including the signaling/TURN server)** and frames the defense as forcing that observer to buffer/store/analyze mostly-junk to find the few real bits — a real-time correlation/selection burden. Dwork–Naor and Client Puzzles **invert every concrete parameter**: their cost falls on the message *sender* via a *solved CPU puzzle* at the application layer; C2's cost falls on an *involuntary passive observer*, is *storage + downstream cryptanalytic effort* forced by decoy volume, and no one solves a puzzle. Rivest's argument is cryptographic indistinguishability, not resource economics; the anonymity literature's "economics" is participation game theory; EMC2 is cross-channel secret-sharing ("observe all channels"), not intra-mesh decoy volume ("store all chunks").

**Verdict: NOVEL-ADJACENT — and the weakest standalone claim.** The economic-defense *lineage* is old (Dwork–Naor 1992 onward) and the chaff *mechanism* is Rivest 1998 / the anonymity cover-traffic line; only the *direction of the asymmetry* (burden an involuntary observer to protect metadata) and the *decoy-volume storage mechanism* are fresh, and this is rhetorical/positioning more than a new technique. Reviewers will cite Dwork–Naor, Rivest, and the HNDL critique **[citation to verify: arXiv:2603.01091 id/authorship/quote unconfirmed — confirm before publication]**. Present C2 as a framing/positioning of C1, own the Dwork–Naor lineage explicitly, and distinguish "waste analysis effort *now*" from "inflate storage for *later*."

### C3 — A uniform cover-chunk transport makes a PQ Double-Ratchet KEM ciphertext effectively free, and PQ rekeys can be hidden in decoy chunk slots
**Closest prior art:** Triple Ratchet (EUROCRYPT 2025, the anchor/comparator) + SPQR (2025) + iMessage PQ3 (RWC 2024 / USENIX Sec 2025) + Auerbach et al. "How to Compare Bandwidth Constrained…" (USENIX Sec 2025) + PQXDH verification (USENIX Sec 2024) + PQ Deniable KE / Signal Handshake (PKC 2022) + X-Wing (IACR CiC 2024, 1120 B) + Alwen–Coretti–Dodis (EUROCRYPT 2019, the CKA soundness enabler); FIPS 203 (fixed CT sizes); pqc-ratchet (a PQ ratchet in a browser, but no transport/chunking angle). **Technical caveat: Kemeleon (RWC 2025).**

**How p2party differs:** Every PQ-ratchet effort found treats the KEM ciphertext as a bandwidth cost to *engineer down* (erasure-coding, amortize-and-throttle, a new smaller KEM like Triple Ratchet's "Katana") — **never** as something that rides free inside pre-existing uniform cover-chunk capacity, and **never** hidden in decoy/noise slots. The primary sources verify the contrast in the authors' own words (2272 B, 71×, 40-B budget, 32-B erasure-coded chunks; X-Wing 1120 B ≈ 1.71% of a 64 KiB chunk). The exact problem EUROCRYPT 2025's Triple Ratchet needed a new erasure-coded protocol and a new KEM to solve is a **non-problem** in a transport already committed to uniform large padded chunks. The sweep found **no** research or deployed system that (a) already has a uniform/padded/decoy chunk transport as its baseline and (b) exploits it to carry a full PQ ciphertext at zero marginal visible cost or (c) hides a PQ rekey in decoy slots.

**Verdict: NOVEL — the paper's strongest, most defensible claim, and the only one that survives the sweep with no preemption in any confirmed venue.** Keep it honest with three points:
1. The "a big ciphertext fits in a big frame" half is nearly an *observation* once you posit 64 KiB uniform frames — its force comes entirely from the synthesis with the C1 transport and the SPQR/PQ3 contrast, so present it as an insight enabled by C1, not a standalone trick.
2. The genuinely non-obvious half is **cover-traffic PQ-rekey hiding in decoy slots** — lead with that, but frame it precisely as **uncrowded / unpreempted in every confirmed venue**, *not* as "first" or "novel-in-principle" (steganographic decoy-slot hiding as a category is old — Provably Secure Steganography, Slitheen).
3. **Kemeleon (RWC 2025) is the sharpest unresolved objection to the decoy-slot sub-claim and must be answered head-on.** Raw ML-KEM public keys and ciphertexts are **not** byte-uniform, so a raw X-Wing/ML-KEM ct placed in a "pure-noise" decoy chunk is **statistically fingerprintable** — an observer can tell which "decoys" are secretly real rekeys under a byte-level test. **Honest response the paper must state:** either (a) apply a **Kemeleon/Elligator-style obfuscation to make the PQ ciphertext byte-uniform *before* hiding it in a decoy slot**, or (b) explicitly **scope the claim** to chunk-level size/timing/ordering uniformity and concede that byte-content uniformity of hidden rekeys requires the Kemeleon encoding. Cite Kemeleon either way.

Fix the 42→32-byte figure and "erasure codes" (not Reed–Solomon) throughout. If any single claim anchors the paper, it is C3.

### C4 — Browser-native realization: Signal's Double Ratchet ported to native browser tech (IndexedDB persistence + non-extractable WebCrypto at-rest keys) as a permissionless E2E mesh from a shared URL
**Closest prior art:** Matrix (Olm/vodozemac) and Wire (Proteus) — **production Double Ratchet in the browser via WASM with IndexedDB persistence**; Wrongthink — a browser app literally advertising "Double Ratchet over DTLS (WebRTC)"; 2key-ratchet — Double Ratchet ported to WebCrypto; positive-intentions — Signal DR over P2P WebRTC in-browser; Corella (2017) — the non-extractable-CryptoKey-in-IndexedDB pattern as a *known general technique*; pqc-ratchet — a *PQ* ratchet already browser-compatible. Definitional/model baseline (one abstraction layer above any implementation, so must-cite but non-preempting): Alwen–Coretti–Dodis (EUROCRYPT 2019), Cohn-Gordon et al. (EuroS&P 2017 / J. Cryptology 2020), Poettering–Rösler (CRYPTO 2018), Jost–Maurer–Mularczyk (EUROCRYPT 2019); SoK: Secure Messaging (S&P 2015), which explicitly scopes endpoint/key-extraction *out*.

**How p2party differs:** The headline "Double Ratchet ported to native browser tech + IndexedDB" is **not** new — it is in production (Matrix, Wire) and in hobby form (Wrongthink, positive-intentions). "DR on WebCrypto" is 2key-ratchet. The non-extractable-key-in-IndexedDB pattern is Corella 2017. The academic-model papers (ACD, Cohn-Gordon) formalize the ratchet as an abstract algorithm with no key-storage substrate, and SoK: Secure Messaging explicitly assumes secure endpoints — so **none of the theory preempts a storage-substrate contribution, and one of them (the SoK) hands us the exact out-of-scope gap C4 fills.** What none of them combine is: a **permissionless mesh from a bare shared URL** (Matrix/Wire need accounts/homeservers; Wrongthink/positive-intentions/2key-ratchet are two-party) **+** non-extractable-WebCrypto keys applied specifically to **live Double-Ratchet chain state at rest** (Matrix/Wire keep keys in WASM memory / encrypted blobs; the browser-ratchet projects have no such story, and none apply Corella's generic pattern to a live ratchet in a WebRTC mesh).

**Verdict: ALREADY-DONE for the headline; NOVEL-ADJACENT only for the specific combination.** Be blunt in the paper: "Double Ratchet in a browser over WebRTC/DTLS" is precedented (cite Wrongthink and positive-intentions to pre-empt the reviewer, and Matrix/Wire for the mature production version). Claim only the *narrow, un-preempted residue*: permissionless-shared-URL mesh **and** non-extractable-WebCrypto sealing of live ratchet chain state persisted in IndexedDB — presented as an engineering integration located exactly in SoK: Secure Messaging's declared-out-of-scope endpoint/key-extraction gap, **not** as a first. (A genuine negative result: no confirmed EUROCRYPT/CRYPTO/S&P/USENIX/CCS/NDSS/PoPETs/RWC paper describes this realization — but the true competition, if any, likely sits in W2SP/blog/engineering write-ups outside the confirmed clusters; see §7.)

### C5 — CPace over the post-DTLS data channel, binding both peers' DTLS certificate fingerprints AND Ed25519 identity keys into the PAKE channel-input, seeding the ratchet root, with a human PIN for MITM resistance without trusting signaling
**Closest prior art:** webwormhole — already runs **CPace binding DTLS certificate fingerprints** in-browser (preempts that exact sub-mechanism); Security Analysis of CPace (ASIACRYPT 2021) — the primitive's UC/adaptive-corruption pedigree, with a *generic opaque* channel-identifier slot C5 fills; Triple Handshakes and Cookie Cutters (S&P 2014) — binding higher-layer auth to the transport session/certificate as an established technique; Universally Composable Relaxed PAKE (CRYPTO 2020) and OPAQUE (EUROCRYPT 2018 / RFC 9807) — the PAKE-landscape pedigree and aPAKE counterpoint; RFC 8827 WebRTC IdP — binds identity to the DTLS fingerprint against a malicious signaling server, but via a trusted IdP; ZRTP — human-SAS MITM defense on the media path; PakeMail — PAKE for decentralized no-TTP trust; Reclaiming our Passwords/Zoom (RWC 2024) — PAKE against a malicious coordination server; CPace draft — anticipates CI/AD binding but leaves it to the app; Magic-Wormhole — PAKE with bound identity strings.

**How p2party differs:** No source — standard, draft, deployed, or flagship-venue paper — runs a balanced PAKE over a **post-handshake WebRTC data channel** with **both** the DTLS certificate fingerprint **and** a persistent Ed25519 identity key folded into the same channel-binding input, requiring **no trusted third party**, and **seeding an ongoing Double Ratchet**. The ASIACRYPT 2021 proof vets CPace itself but treats the channel identifier as environment-supplied auxiliary data and never instantiates it. Triple Handshakes establishes the *general* transport-binding principle, but is a TLS attack/fix paper, not a PAKE-input construction. webwormhole does CPace+fingerprint but binds no identity key, runs over signaling (not the post-DTLS data channel), and has no persistent ratchet. RFC 8827 achieves fingerprint↔identity binding only via a trusted IdP and only "cannot forge" (still strippable). ZRTP uses SAS not a PAKE and never touches a certificate fingerprint.

**Verdict: NOVEL-ADJACENT.** The dual-binding (fingerprint + identity) + ratchet-seeding + PIN-without-IdP combination is not found in any confirmed venue, but each piece is prior art and webwormhole preempts the CPace+fingerprint core. Claim the *dual-binding + ratchet-root-seeding + IdP-free PIN* synthesis, citing CPace's ASIACRYPT proof as the primitive's pedigree and Triple Handshakes as the channel-binding precedent, and explicitly distinguishing webwormhole (the mechanism) and RFC 8827 (the trust-anchor argument). (Nearest remaining prior art to rule out lives in TLS channel-binding RFCs — see §7.)

## 5. Positioning statement

**The single sharpest defensible contribution is C3: the observation-and-mechanism that a uniform, decoy-capable cover-chunk transport makes a post-quantum Double-Ratchet's large KEM ciphertext effectively free to carry — and lets a PQ rekey be hidden in a decoy chunk slot — in explicit, primary-source-verified contrast to Signal's SPQR (32-byte erasure-coded micro-chunks, 40-byte/message budget, 2272-byte ML-KEM payload) and Apple's PQ3 (throttled visible size spikes), and to EUROCRYPT 2025's Triple Ratchet (which needed a new erasure-coded protocol *and* a smaller KEM to afford what p2party affords for free).** This is the only claim that survives the sweep as **NOVEL** with no preemption in any confirmed venue. It is anchored by verified numbers (2272 B / 71× / 40-B budget / 32-B chunks; X-Wing 1120 B ≈ 1.71% of a 64 KiB chunk) and its one open technical dependency — byte-uniformity of a hidden PQ rekey — is met by adopting a **Kemeleon (RWC 2025)** encoding or by scoping the claim to size/timing uniformity. The decoy-slot half is **uncrowded / unpreempted**, not "first."

The paper's honest shape is therefore: **C1 (the uniform-chunk-flood browser transport) as the enabling system contribution (NOVEL-ADJACENT), C3 as the crypto insight it uniquely unlocks (NOVEL — lead with the decoy-slot rekey-hiding, framed as uncrowded, and answer Kemeleon), C5 as a supporting handshake contribution (NOVEL-ADJACENT), with C2 framed as C1's threat-model narrative and C4 presented candidly as engineering integration whose novel residue is narrow.** Do not sell C4 as a first, and do not sell C1/C2/C5 as more than prior-art-contested syntheses.

**Venue class:** applied privacy/systems security — **PoPETs** (best fit: metadata-privacy + applied crypto + real system) or **WPES**; a hardened systems build-out could target **USENIX Security / NDSS**-adjacent applied tracks. The C3 insight alone, expanded with a measurement of "PQ ciphertext cost vs. SPQR/PQ3 across a real WebRTC mesh," is a plausible standalone PoPETs paper. Real World Crypto is a natural talk venue given the Signal/Apple contrast (and the Kemeleon/CFRG audience for the decoy-slot obfuscation dependency).
