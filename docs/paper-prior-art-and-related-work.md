# p2party — Related Work & Prior-Art / Novelty Assessment

_Honest source material for an applied/systems cryptography paper. Not marketing. Its job is to decide what the paper may legitimately claim, cite the closest prior art for each claim, and concede where a claim is already done._

_This is the **unified** memo: the original topical prior-art pass merged with a venue-targeted supplement whose flagship-venue hits were confirmed venue+year against DBLP and IACR ePrint. Where a work appeared in both passes it is stated once, with the venue-confirmed attribution. See the note at the head of §6._

---

## 1. Framing

p2party is an **applied/systems** contribution, not a primitive-level novelty. Nothing here invents a new cryptographic primitive, a new PAKE, a new KEM, or a new ratchet security notion — those are settled literature and the paper explicitly does **not** claim the Double Ratchet mechanism as novel. What protocol-v3 currently implements is a **specific engineering synthesis**: a browser-native, permissionless, full pairwise WebRTC/DTLS mesh with 65,490-byte active-transfer cells, random in-cell offsets/fill, decoy cells, Merkle-authenticated reassembly, and exact 65-byte tagged true-or-random receipt frames. Every edge bootstraps from online interactive X25519 3DH plus one exact room-selected FIPS 203 ML-KEM-512/768/1024 profile; PIN policy additionally contributes the draft-21 CPace ISK. Three chained confirmation proofs bind the transcript before the initiator establishes. Ed25519 remains the classical identity anchor and cross-signs a dedicated X25519 identity key. An internal sparse PQ-healing state-machine core now exists, but production nonzero epochs, scheduled idle-time cover, L2 rendezvous, and P2BT remain unshipped until persistence/transport/scheduler integration and evaluation land.

Almost every _individual ingredient_ has decades of precedent. The defensible contribution is therefore the **combination, its browser/WebRTC realization, and the measured trade-off of sparse PQ ratcheting against a room-wide fixed-cell schedule**. An earlier pass labelled C3 (put a PQ rekey into otherwise scheduled cover capacity) the one primitive-like novel insight. The independent 2026-07-23 adjudication below retracts that broad claim: adjacent fixed-packet PQ systems and a same-day public implementation make “first” or “unpreempted” indefensible. What remains may still be paper-worthy as an applied privacy/systems result, but only if the implementation and evaluation establish an architecture-specific advantage.

Two figure-precision fixes that the paper must make regardless of framing:

- **The "~42-byte SPQR chunk" figure is wrong** and must be corrected. Source-verified: SPQR's shipped constant is `CHUNK_SIZE = 32` bytes (`src/encoding/polynomial.rs`); Signal's production per-message post-quantum PCS _budget_ is **40 bytes/message** (Auerbach et al., NIST PQC 2025); the Signal blog's illustrative walkthrough uses **100-byte** chunks pedagogically; the PQShield secondary source says "42-byte" but is **not authoritative and must not be trusted over the 32-byte source constant**. This whole document is internally consistent on **32** (not 42). SPQR erasure-codes ML-KEM-768's encapsulation key (1184 B → ~37 chunks) and ciphertext (1088 B → ~34 chunks) into 32-byte chunks; the fragmentation family is **erasure codes** (Signal's own wording) — **not** "Reed–Solomon," which no primary source attributes to SPQR.
- **ML-KEM-768 EK+CT = 2272 bytes = ~71× the 32-byte classical DH message** — verified word-for-word in the Auerbach et al. primary source. FIPS 203 gives the actual separate sizes: 1,184-byte encapsulation key and 1,088-byte ciphertext. Either object fits comfortably inside one current 65,490-byte p2party cell; together they are not a single ciphertext and they are not “free.” **X-Wing's 1,120-byte ciphertext is only a comparator:** the construction was published in IACR CiC 2024, while its current protocol document is active individual Internet-Draft 10, not an RFC. X-Wing is a non-interactive, unauthenticated KEM and is not the p2party handshake.

## 1.1 Independent adjudication of the 2026-07-22 pass (2026-07-23)

This section records a second-pass judgment of the Claude-produced survey against a broader crypto **and networking/anonymity** search. It supersedes every older sentence in this memo that calls C3 “the only NOVEL claim,” “unpreempted,” or a first.

### What the first pass got right

- It correctly rejected primitive-level novelty. Double/Triple Ratchets, ML-KEM, CKA composition, fixed-size padding, cover traffic, PAKE channel binding, and browser ratchets all have substantial prior art.
- It found the right secure-messaging comparators: Signal SPQR/Triple Ratchet, Apple PQ3, PQXDH, X-Wing, and the formal ratchet literature. It also corrected two material source errors: SPQR uses **32-byte** chunks, not the secondary-source “42-byte” figure, and Signal says **erasure codes**, not specifically Reed–Solomon.
- It correctly recognized that a paper needs an explicit observer model, real packet traces, and attack baselines rather than a diagram-level “all chunks look the same” argument.
- It usefully found Kemeleon/obfuscated KEMs, which prevent us from claiming that making raw PQ key material look random is new.

### Surviving paper direction

The candidate result is now deliberately narrower:

> Can a **server-blind n-party control plane** privately assemble p2party's shipped browser-native WebRTC/DTLS full peer mesh, and can sparse authenticated hybrid PQ ratchet advances then substitute for already scheduled, room-wide fixed cells—rather than paying a permanent per-frame KEM tax—while one ephemeral `RTCDataChannel` per logical message preserves independent cancellation, progress, backpressure, retry, and teardown?

That is an architecture-specific research question, not a new cryptographic primitive and not yet a result. The implementation direction is:

1. Keep two boundaries explicit. The implemented **bootstrap** currently combines interactive 3DH and the exact room-selected ML-KEM-512/768/1024 profile (plus CPace ISK in PIN rooms) into one root under an application-specific, domain-separated, key-confirmed transcript; it needs a proof or an explicit engineered-hybrid assumption. The future **periodic PQ healing** should use a standard Triple-Ratchet/Sparse-CKA-style composition with separate classical/PQ chains and a domain-separated message-key combiner.
2. Authenticate the canonical room cover policy and version during handshake/key confirmation. Immediate/no-cover remains the product default; cover is room-wide, mismatch fails closed, and schedule changes occur only at pre-announced future boundaries.
3. In a cover room, place sparse PQ control in an already scheduled cell and keep the cell's send/receipt/close behavior unchanged. A cancelled admitted lane becomes cover until scheduled close.
4. Keep the raw ML-KEM record within the authenticated application encryption and DTLS envelope. Revisit Kemeleon/hybrid OKEM only if a future wire layer exposes KEM bytes or claims protocol-looking-like-random before DTLS.
5. Treat large files separately: base-lane transfer is slow but most hidden; fixed rate/duration classes leak the class but hide exact size/progress/cancel; immediate bulk exposes activity. Hiding a fast bulk transfer requires demand-independent dummy bulk capacity.

**Closest formal handshake baseline — PQXDH.** Signal's PQXDH specification and
its USENIX Security 2024 analysis support the familiar property list only
**collectively and within their models**. ProVerif revision 2 symbolically proves
three maximal-compromise authentication/secrecy queries from which forward
secrecy, HNDL resistance, KCI resistance, and session independence are derived.
CryptoVerif's computational result is narrower: classical message secrecy and
peer authentication under GapDH/random-oracle/AEAD/EUF-CMA assumptions, plus a
post-quantum future-secrecy game under an IND-CCA KEM and PRF assumption with
only the modeled long-term compromise. Neither artifact proves Signal's Double
Ratchet, Sesame, deniability, implementation correctness, or the whole deployed
ecosystem. This corrects the earlier shorthand “PQXDH already has
ProVerif/CryptoVerif analysis covering everything”: the named properties are a
fair aggregate summary, not a statement that both tools independently prove
every property.

PQXDH is designed for asynchronous messaging: an offline recipient publishes
classical and KEM prekeys to a server. p2party instead has both peers online on
a live WebRTC edge, proves possession through interactive 3DH, and can add a
room PIN through CPace. That architecture fit avoids prekey
depletion/replay/substitution machinery and is useful systems specialization;
it is not evidence of superior cryptography. PQXDH has the stronger formal
assurance today. Both retain classical authentication in their current forms:
PQXDH explicitly provides no authentication guarantee against an active
quantum adversary, and p2party still anchors identity in Ed25519.

**p2party proof boundary found—and immediately hardened—in the audit.** The
0.10 two-confirmation ordering allowed either confirmation MAC to be modified
so one endpoint could complete while the other rejected. The 0.11 working tree
now chains three proofs: responder `mac_R`; initiator
`mac_I = MAC(transcript || mac_R)`; responder
`mac_F = MAC(transcript || mac_R || mac_I)`. The initiator does not establish
until `mac_F` verifies. Consequently a modified `mac_R` poisons `mac_I`, a
modified `mac_I` prevents `mac_F`, and a modified `mac_F` is rejected.

The precise remaining boundary is delivery of the last packet:
`AcceptI ⇒ VerifyI_R ∧ FinishSentR`, and responder completion implies it
verified the initiator proof, but `AcceptR ⇏ AcceptI` if the valid FINISH is
dropped after the responder's send succeeds. This is the unavoidable
finite-handshake/common-knowledge edge, not key disclosure. An open WebRTC
DataChannel is not a substitute application acknowledgement: it establishes
DTLS/SCTP before p2party verifies PIN, identity, KEM transcript, or ratchet root,
and JavaScript `send()` exposes no “peer verified this MAC” receipt.

The smallest useful first model is the no-PIN, pairwise, exact-suite handshake
with arbitrary agents/sessions and an independently authenticated Ed25519
identity anchor. ProVerif should cover root secrecy, injective contextual
agreement, KCI, post-session static-key forward secrecy, HNDL,
downgrade/UKS/key-confusion, and channel binding. Tamarin is the better next
tool for confirmation ordering, erasure timing, skipped keys, ratchet state,
and the distinction between pairwise edge agreement and room-wide group
agreement. CryptoVerif must not idealize away the open combiner question:
`HKDF-Extract(0, [CPace-ISK ||] 3DH || ML-KEMss)` still needs an explicit
multi-source/QROM robust-combiner argument. The PQXDH Kyber SH-CR result cannot
simply be relabelled as a FIPS ML-KEM theorem; standardized ML-KEM is not
byte- or algorithm-compatible with pre-standard Kyber.

**X-Wing boundary.** Draft-10 defines a concrete X25519+ML-KEM-768 KEM combiner; it is neither interactive nor mutually authenticated and it is still an Internet-Draft without RFC standing. It is valuable combiner prior art and a possible future component, not the name or proof of p2party's multi-input handshake.

**Current signaling boundary and expanded scope:** p2party's full WebRTC data mesh is discovered through a centralized signaling star. The shipped client sends the server the 64-character `roomUrl` with a stable `peerId`, requests the roster by server-assigned `roomId`, receives peer IDs/public keys, and routes SDP/ICE for every pair through that service. High-entropy and PIN rooms make discovery unguessable and peer authentication stronger; they do **not** hide co-membership from the server. The server is blind to E2E content and in-band secret material, not to the room graph. The maintainer has now folded strong L2 membership blindness into the combined target. A common opaque/OPRF token is insufficient because it remains a co-membership handle; the selected construction must privatize both discovery and encrypted fixed-size SDP/ICE exchange. Protocol-level graph blindness must be distinguished from source-IP/timing/TURN unlinkability.

### Evidence required before calling it a finding

- Real Chromium/Firefox/Safari captures for direct and TURN paths, including SCTP/DTLS fragmentation, DCEP channel-open/close records, retransmission, loss, and congestion.
- A classifier experiment over idle, text, cancellation, attachment, sparse-PQ-advance, and dummy epochs. The relevant result is indistinguishability **at the declared observer layer**, not equality of application buffers.
- Latency, bandwidth, CPU, battery, TURN cost, and channel-churn measurements for the no-cover/10 s/15 s/30 s/60 s/2 min/5 min/10 min profiles. The shipped WebRTC data plane is a full mesh with `n(n−1)/2` edges and `n−1` edges per endpoint; the centralized signaling/bootstrap service is the star, not a data hub.
- A direct comparison with (a) Signal SPQR/Triple Ratchet's amortized PQ budget, (b) Apple PQ3's visible throttled rekeys, and (c) Zerion's per-frame ML-KEM + constant-rate 4096-byte Tor stream.
- Adversarial tests for downgrade, policy mismatch, schedule acceleration, cancellation/completion leakage, cell overflow, epoch retry/idempotence, replay/fork detection, crash-consistent serialization, and “PQ authentication” overclaiming. The current Ed25519/X25519 identity path gives hybrid/PQ **confidentiality**, not post-quantum authentication.
- A separate signaling-server leakage table. Do not let cover-traffic results imply that the current server cannot enumerate room members: it can.
- L2 evaluation against Myco, Peer2PIR, Pung/Talek/Riposte/Vuvuzela/Stadium/Karaoke/Alpenhorn and the PIR/private-write literature: server CPU/storage, client download/trial-decryption cost, joining latency, anonymity-set size, epoch churn, spam resistance, record loss, and what the server can link from IP/timing. The self-hosted service must remain operationally plausible.

**Current academic verdict:** the already implemented hybrid bootstrap, n-party pairwise browser mesh, message-scoped channels, active-transfer cells, and durable ratchet are a credible artifact base, but they are not by themselves a new cryptographic result. The combined L2 rendezvous + sparse-PQ scheduled-mesh architecture is worth building and evaluating as an applied privacy/systems paper; today it is a research direction, not a finding. **WPES or PoPETs** is the realistic first target. A stronger USENIX Security/NDSS submission would require a finished public deployment, an explicit leakage/security argument for both control and data planes, convincing packet-level/classifier results, and either a proof of the bootstrap composition or a deliberately narrower claim.

## 2. Related Work, by dimension

_Type is marked `[R]` research, `[I]` in-the-wild implementation, `[S]` standard/draft._

### A. Traffic-analysis-resistant transports, cover traffic & padding (targets C1/C2)

**Research.**

- **Chaffing and Winnowing** (Rivest, 1998) `[R]` — the conceptual ancestor of C2: a receiver strips MAC-verified "wheat" from indistinguishable "chaff"; an eavesdropper without the key must consider everything. This is _the_ precedent for "confidentiality/effort via authentication, not hiding content." Note it is unencrypted-packet-level and explicitly permits _unshuffled_ chaff; it has no notion of uniform framing, traffic-analysis, or a networked transport.
- **Tarzan** (Freedman & Morris, CCS 2002) `[R]` — closest P2P architectural ancestor: always-on, fixed-size, encrypted "mimic" cover-traffic links between peers, indistinguishable from real payload, with real data opportunistically substituted. Link-level, not per-message chunk-level; per-hop MAC not Merkle; single-path source-routed, not shuffle-and-flood.
- **Sphinx: A Compact and Provably Secure Mix Format** (Danezis & Goldberg, IEEE S&P 2009) `[R]` — the flagship-venue statement that a **bitwise- and length-uniform packet defeats a traffic observer**, and it itself traces the uniform-packaging idea to Chaum 1981. A **novelty threat to C1's general principle** (uniformity as a defense is old). But Sphinx is store-and-forward mix-relay, all-or-nothing (LIONESS/SPRP whole-packet cipher, no partial verification), and explicitly excludes decoy/economic defenses — the last of which _supports_ the C2 contrast. Cite up front as the general-principle ancestor.
- **PURBs / Padmé** (Nikitin et al., PoPETs 2019) `[R]` — the nearest academic "uniform ciphertext" concept, but for a **single static blob** (padding to a size lattice; length leakage → O(log log M) bits). The paper _explicitly disclaims_ the time-distribution-of-multiple-objects problem. No chunking, decoys, flood, or Merkle-auth. A **novelty threat to C1's ciphertext-shape principle** — a PoPETs reviewer's first citation.
- **A Systematic Approach to Developing and Evaluating Website Fingerprinting Defenses (Tamaraw)** (Cai, Nithyanand, Wang, Johnson, Goldberg, ACM CCS 2014) `[R]` — the canonical **constant-rate, padded-to-uniform** WF defense, and **C1's single sharpest prior-art threat**: a CCS/NDSS traffic-analysis reviewer opens with it. It is single-Tor-circuit, packet-granularity, with no decoy chunks, no shuffle-across-peers, no Merkle partial verification, and a censorship/DPI (not DTLS-mesh) threat model. Relates head-on; does not preempt the composition.
- **Maybenot: A Framework for Traffic Analysis Defenses** (Pulls & Witwer, WPES @ CCS 2023) `[R]` — a generic framework for **scheduling padding**. A novelty threat only if C1 is framed as "a padding framework"; it is not — distinguish explicitly.
- **DeTorrent: An Adversarial Padding-only Traffic Analysis Defense** (Holland, Carpenter, Oh, Hopper, PoPETs 2024) `[R]` and **RegulaTor: A Straightforward Website Fingerprinting Defense** (Holland & Hopper, PoPETs 2022) `[R]` — padding-only WF defenses are a **crowded, current line** (RegulaTor→DeTorrent lineage). C1 must not be sold as "another padding defense."
- **Deep Fingerprinting** (Sirinam, Imani, Juarez, Wright, ACM CCS 2018) `[R]` and **Automated Website Fingerprinting through Deep Learning** (Rimmer, Preuveneers, Juarez, van Goethem, Joosen, NDSS 2018) `[R]` — the **deep-learning attack baselines** any C1 evaluation must beat; not competing defenses. The measurement section must report against these, not just count/size/timing heuristics.
- **ScrambleSuit** (Winter, Pulls, Fuss, WPES 2013) `[R]`, **Walkie-Talkie** (Wang & Goldberg, USENIX Sec 2017) `[R]`, **FRONT** (Gong & Wang, USENIX Sec 2020) `[R]`, **FTE** (Dyer, Coull, Ristenpart, Shrimpton, CCS 2013) `[R]`, **Telex / decoy routing** (Wustrow et al., USENIX Sec 2011) `[R]` — the WF-defense / obfsproxy / decoy-routing family. All explore "pad + decoy + shape-morph to defeat count/size/timing" but at packet or page-load granularity over a **single Tor circuit / 1:1 tunnel**, for a censorship/DPI threat model, not a multi-peer E2E WebRTC mesh. Telex's "decoy" is a decoy _destination_, a different word entirely — worth one clarifying citation so the paper does not conflate the two "decoy" literatures.
- **Traffic Morphing** (Wright, Coull, Monrose, NDSS 2009) `[R]` — the canonical **opposite** economic philosophy to C2: reshape one traffic class to look like another at _minimal_ overhead. The paper must engage this to justify choosing the expensive-for-both regime.

**In-the-wild / standards.**

- **obfs4** (Yawning Angel / Tor Project, 2014–) `[I]` — deployed at scale; makes every byte statistically indistinguishable from random (Elligator2 + SipHash-masked lengths). "Looks random" via masking of a single client-bridge stream, not uniform padded decoy chunks + Merkle + flood.
- **WebRTC Data Channels / DCEP** (RFCs 8831 and 8832) `[S]` — DataChannels run as SCTP streams over DTLS, while channel OPEN/ACK and stream-reset operations are themselves encrypted records with lifecycle, fragmentation, retransmission, and congestion effects. This is why p2party may call its 65,490-byte application cells equal length but may not call direct/TURN packet traces indistinguishable before capture-and-classifier evidence.

### B. Metadata-private / anonymity messaging systems & the economic-cost lineage (targets C1/C2)

**Research (mostly server-mediated mix / DP-noise lineage).**

- **Loopix** (Piotrowska et al., USENIX Sec 2017) `[R]` — the canonical cover-traffic-defeats-a-global-timing-adversary system (Poisson mix + loop/drop cover, Sphinx fixed-size packets). Closest "indistinguishable junk" precedent for C2's _intuition_, but framed as statistical/information-theoretic indistinguishability, **and it taxes the honest sender's bandwidth — the inverse of C2's cost model**; dedicated mix-server infrastructure, not a browser P2P link.
- **Vuvuzela** (van den Hooff et al., SOSP 2015) `[R]`, **Stadium** (Tyagi et al., SOSP 2017) `[R]`, **Karaoke** (Lazar, Gilad, Zeldovich, OSDI 2018) `[R]` — the differential-privacy dead-drop-noise family. Fixed-size (256-byte) messages + injected noise + mix-shuffle, but justified by formal (ε,δ)-DP bounds on server-observable metadata, requiring a trusted anytrust/honest-majority server federation. Wrong layer (inter-server links, not DTLS records) and wrong trust model (p2party trusts _no_ relay).
- **XRD** (Kwon, Lu, Devadas, NSDI 2020) `[R]` — cryptographic (not DP) privacy via parallel mixnets; explicitly aims to _avoid_ wasted bandwidth — again the opposite of C2.
- **Riposte** (Corrigan-Gibbs, Boneh, Mazières, IEEE S&P 2015) `[R]` — metadata-private write-only messaging via a PIR/DC-net "private information storage," scaling to millions of users. Different trust model (server-set, reverse-PIR), but a flagship-venue "impose global-observation cost" neighbor worth situating against.
- **Pung** (Angel & Setty, OSDI 2016) `[R]` — titled _Unobservable Communication over **Fully Untrusted Infrastructure**_: the one system that makes **fully untrusted infrastructure** do genuinely expensive work (computational PIR over the whole DB), the closest "impose cost" precedent. But the cost lands on the untrusted PIR **server's CPU**, not on a passive DTLS eavesdropper's storage.
- **Myco** (Kaviani, Rathee, Annem, Popa, IEEE S&P 2025) `[R]` — the current efficiency baseline the earlier pass missed. Myco replaces the traditional quadratic PIR bottleneck with an oblivious data structure in an asymmetric two-server distributed-trust model, achieving `O(N log² N)` work and reporting up to 302× throughput over multi-server and 2,219× over single-server PIR systems. It is asynchronous messaging rather than ephemeral WebRTC-room assembly, but it prevents describing Talek as the strongest practical metadata-private substrate without qualification.
- **Dissent** (Corrigan-Gibbs & Ford, CCS 2010) `[R]` and **Herbivore** (Goel et al., Cornell TR 2003) `[R]` — DC-net-derived P2P ancestors where members transmit every round regardless of real data. Genuine "waste-by-construction" pedigree — but both **explicitly optimize _away_ wasted bits** (Dissent: "none of these bits are wasted padding"; Herbivore reserves slots only for actual senders and criticizes constant-rate P5 for wasting bandwidth). They are the _contrast_ to C2, not its precedent.
- **Pricing via Processing or Combatting Junk Mail** (Dwork & Naor, CRYPTO 1992) `[R]` — the **origin of the cost-asymmetric defense** and the root C2's "offensive cryptography" framing should own. It **inverts every concrete parameter of C2**: the cost falls on the message _sender_ (to earn acceptance), taxes _CPU per message via a solved puzzle_, and is an application-layer anti-abuse gate. C2 taxes a _passive observer_ on _storage + downstream cryptanalytic effort_, and no one solves a puzzle. The novelty is the _direction_ of the asymmetry, not the abstraction.
- **Client Puzzles: A Cryptographic Countermeasure Against Connection Depletion** (Juels & Brainard, NDSS 1999) `[R]` — the same "impose asymmetric cost on the adversary" principle applied to DoS (compute, not storage). Historical root; not a competing mechanism.
- **EMC2: Johnny Should Use Secret Sharing** (Chandran, Demuth, Edalatnejad, Linsner, Reuter, Schneider, WPES @ CCS 2024) `[R]` — the closest _surveillance-cost_ argument to C2: an adversary must observe **all** channels to reconstruct anything (n-of-n secret sharing across independent external channels). Distinguish precisely from C2's "must **store** all chunks within one mesh" (decoy/padding volume, not cross-channel sharing).
- **TEEMS: A TEE-based Metadata-protected Messaging System** (Sasy, Johnson, Goldberg, PoPETs 2025) `[R]` — a must-cite contrast for _why p2party avoids TEEs_ and uses decoy-volume storage economics instead.
- **Alpenhorn** (Lazar & Zeldovich, OSDI 2016) `[R]` — metadata-private bootstrapping/friend-discovery via anytrust IBE servers; same goal as C5 but server-trusting, not a peer PAKE.
- **On the Economics of Anonymity** (Acquisti, Dingledine, Syverson, FC 2003) `[R]` — the field's canonical "economics" paper, cited precisely to show its "economics" means _participant-incentive game theory_, a different sense than C2's "impose cost on the eavesdropper."
- **Strong Anonymity for Mesh Messaging** (Perry, Spang, Eskandarian, Boneh, arXiv 2022) `[R]` — the literature's nearest "P2P mesh + metadata privacy" neighbor. But Bluetooth radio (not WebRTC), whole-message-granularity fixed-size cover traffic + anti-entropy gossip, classical ElGamal signcryption, no ratchet/PQ, QR/web-of-trust bootstrap (not PAKE).
- **SoK: Metadata-Protecting Communication Systems** (Sasy & Goldberg, PoPETs 2024) `[R]` — the **taxonomy any "we use cover traffic for metadata privacy" claim is measured against**, and a **novelty threat to C1/C2's framing**: a privacy reviewer will place p2party in this landscape and ask which cell is new (answer: browser-native, serverless P2P mesh + PQ ratchet — a cell the SoK does not cover).
- **The Last Hop Attack: Why Loop Cover Traffic over Fixed Cascades Threatens Anonymity** (Weisenseel, Döpmann, Tschorsch, PoPETs 2025) `[R]` — state-of-the-art cover-traffic **attack**; reviewers will ask whether the shuffle-and-flood mesh (not a fixed cascade) is vulnerable to this class. The evaluation must answer it.
- **Generic Anonymity Wrapper for Messaging Protocols** (Thiemt, Rösler, Bienstock, Schmidt, Dodis; ACM CCS 2025, "A Practical Wrapper Protocol for Metadata-Hiding," RWC 2026 talk) `[R]` — the closest current "anonymity-in-messaging" thread; it hides _identity/metadata_ at a wrapper layer, complementary to (not competing with) C1/C2's traffic-pattern shaping.

**In-the-wild.**

- **Signal Sealed Sender** (Signal, 2018) `[I]` + **Improving Signal's Sealed Sender** (Martiny et al., NDSS 2021) `[R]` — hides only sender identity from the server; a statistical-disclosure attack on predictable delivery-receipt timing de-anonymizes senders. **Motivates rather than preempts C1**, and specifically validates p2party's design choice to make **reverse read-receipts uniform-size** — closing exactly the predictable-receipt loophole this attack exploited. (Correction to earlier framing: real Signal already bucket-pads to multiples of 160 bytes, and the NDSS'21 attack explicitly excludes group messaging and network metadata — do not overstate it.)

### C. Steganographic / covert-channel & decoy-slot techniques (targets C1 + the C3 decoy-rekey half)

**Research.**

- **Slitheen** (Bocovich & Goldberg, CCS 2016) `[R]` — the closest "only the keyholder can pick the real bytes out of what looks like ordinary cover traffic" technique: overwrites an innocuous decoy site's download with covert data, statistically identical in size/timing/count. Network/decoy-routing layer (imitating a specific overt destination), not a uniform-64 KiB P2P mesh. No PQ/rekey angle.
- **Slitheen++: Stealth TLS-based Decoy Routing** (Birtel & Rossow, USENIX FOCI 2020) `[R]` — "defeat a TLS/DTLS-record observer" at a different layer; a contrast citation for the record-observer threat model.
- **Decoy Routing** (Karlin et al., FOCI 2011) `[R]` — foundational "a connection carries a hidden signal invisible to the observer but actionable by a cooperating in-path party." ~15-year lineage (Telex/Cirripede/TapDance/Rebound/Slitheen) the paper should acknowledge when framing C1's decoy-detection as new. **Terminology-collision warning:** FOCI's "decoy routing" ≠ p2party's "decoy chunk" — preempt reviewer confusion.
- **Provably Secure Steganography** (Hopper, Langford, von Ahn, CRYPTO 2002) `[R]` — the closest **theory anchor** for decoy-chunk indistinguishability (general steganographic security, not chunk/DTLS-specific). Bears on C1 and the C3 decoy-slot half; cite as the formal backdrop against which "hide a rekey in a decoy slot" is defined. This is also why the C3 decoy-slot half must be framed as _uncrowded_, not _first-in-principle_.
- **Learning to Behave: Improving Covert Channel Security with Behavior-Based Designs** (Wails, Stange, Troper, Caliskan, Dingledine, Jansen, Sherr, PoPETs 2022) `[R]` — covert-channel hiding literature; p2party's uniform chunks sidestep the behavioral-realism problem it tackles. Contrast citation.
- **Deniable file systems** — StegFS (McDonald & Kuhn, IH 1999), Rubberhose, TrueCrypt hidden volumes `[R]` — same chaff/decoy-indistinguishability idea but **at rest** (disk blocks), a different problem (coercion-resistant storage). Does not preempt any of C1–C5.
- **On the Practical Feasibility of Harvest-Now, Decrypt-Later Attacks** (Blanco-Romero et al., arXiv:2603.01091, 2026) `[R/preprint]` — the verified critique the paper must pre-empt for C2: its abstract says storage inflation penalizes both sides while quantum-cost inflation targets the adversary, making rekeying and larger key-exchange parameters stronger HNDL levers. **Different threat model:** it models future-decryption economics across TLS/QUIC/SSH, whereas p2party's cover hypothesis targets real-time activity classification and archive selection. Do not turn that distinction into a claim that storage padding is cryptographically novel.

**In-the-wild.**

- **BitTorrent MSE/PE** (Azureus/Vuze community, 2006–) `[I]` — the closest deployed P2P precedent for padding/decoys: inserts obfuscating padding messages into a swarm to hide protocol structure. But **variable-size** (RC4-obfuscated), no Merkle, no uniform framing — and independent fingerprinting research identifies it at >96% accuracy from packet size/timing. A "prior attempt that failed for lack of uniformity" — it _strengthens_ p2party's uniform-chunk rationale.

### D. Post-quantum ratchets, fixed-packet transports, obfuscated KEMs & large-KEM-ciphertext transport (targets C3, with C4 model support)

**Research — PQ-ratchet transport (verifies the Signal-like bandwidth contrast, but is not the whole adjacent field).**

- **Post-Quantum Ratcheting for Signal** (Auerbach, Dodis, Jost, Katsumata, Prest, Schmidt, NIST PQC 2025) `[R]` — full-text-verified primary source. ML-KEM EK+CT = 2272 B = "71 times longer" than 32-B DH; production caps PCS overhead at **40 B/message**; the Sparse-CKA contribution erasure-codes the big handshake into small chunks (tested 32/128/512 B) _because production bandwidth cannot absorb 2272 B at once_. **Zero mention** anywhere in 37 pages of cover traffic, decoys, or hiding rekeys from a metadata observer.
- **The Triple Ratchet: A Bandwidth Efficient Hybrid-Secure Signal Protocol** (Dodis, Jost, Katsumata, Prest, Schmidt, EUROCRYPT 2025, pp. 302–331) `[R]` — **the C3 anchor/comparator.** State-of-the-art PQ-ratchet transport fix on a _message-oriented_ transport is _still_ **erasure-coding across many small messages + a new smaller KEM ("Katana," 2272→1416 bytes)** — i.e. it solves the problem _the hard way_ precisely because Signal's transport has no uniform-large-chunk cover primitive to exploit. States plainly a naive one-shot PQ send is "an unacceptable size increase," and that PQ3 does "many consecutive re-transmissions of the same (long!) Kyber public keys and ciphertexts." Confirms the entire research line exists to solve a bandwidth-amortization problem p2party sidesteps by construction.
- **How to Compare Bandwidth Constrained Two-Party Secure Messaging Protocols** (Auerbach, Dodis, Jost, Katsumata, Schmidt, USENIX Security 2025) `[R]` — the field treats **"bandwidth budget per message" as the central axis** — the very constraint C3 dissolves. Same-period, top-tier evidence the constraint is real.
- **A Formal Analysis of Apple's iMessage PQ3 Protocol** (Linker, Sasse, Basin, USENIX Security 2025) `[R]` — models KEM material sent in-band on the ratchet's schedule; threat model is confidentiality/authentication, not traffic analysis. Leaves the cover-chunk angle untouched.
- **Formal Verification of the PQXDH Protocol** (Bhargavan, Jacomme, Kiefer, Schmidt, USENIX Security 2024) `[R]` — the closest formal bootstrap baseline. The aggregate ProVerif/CryptoVerif result supports authentication, secrecy, forward secrecy, HNDL resistance, KCI resistance, and session independence under stated models, but the tools prove different scopes; neither proves the Double Ratchet, deniability, implementation, or whole Signal stack. The public artifact audited here is pinned at commit `a09439cc350629ec430fb486305baa4001abbee1` (ProVerif 2.04/CryptoVerif 2.08pl1). PQXDH serves the asynchronous/offline case through a server-hosted prekey directory; p2party instead has two live peers on a WebRTC edge and can add CPace PIN authentication. PQXDH has higher formal assurance today. Both authenticate classically in their current forms; PQXDH explicitly gives no authentication guarantee against an active quantum attacker.
- **Post-quantum Asynchronous Deniable Key Exchange and the Signal Handshake** (Brendel, Fiedler, Günther, Janson, Stebila, PKC 2022) `[R]` — the formal PQ X3DH analogue; **transport-agnostic** (never sizes or carries the ciphertext). (See §7 Gaps for the hedged "internal construction named SPQR" claim, which the paper must _not_ assert as fact.)
- **A Deniability Analysis of Signal's Initial Handshake PQXDH** (Fiedler & Janson, PoPETs 2024) `[R]` — PQXDH deniability context; silent on transport/sizes. Background for the handshake-deniability discussion.

**Research — adjacent fixed-packet and obfuscated-KEM work missed by the first pass.**

- **Post Quantum Sphinx** (Stainton, ePrint 2023/1960) `[R/preprint]` — implements PQ variants of the fixed Sphinx mix packet, including **KEM Sphinx**, explicitly trading a larger fixed header for PQ KEM performance. It is not a ratcheted messenger and does not claim “replace a dummy cell,” but it directly refutes the broad statement that PQ KEM material has never been absorbed by a fixed packet format.
- **Outfox: a Postquantum Packet Format for Layered Mixnets** (Rial, Piotrowska, Halpin, WPES 2025; earlier arXiv:2412.19937) `[R]` — a KEM-based packet format for layered mixnets with fixed-length routes and PQ instantiations. Unlike Sphinx, Outfox removes per-layer padding and its packet shrinks as layers are processed, so it is **not** a direct constant-size-cover precedent. It is still mandatory adjacent networking prior art and another reason not to claim that PQ KEM packet formats are absent.
- **Obfuscated Key Exchange** (Günther, Stebila, Veitch, CCS 2024; ePrint 2024/1086) `[R]` — formalizes key exchange whose transcript looks random, proves obfs4 in the model, and builds a quantum-safe protocol using **Kemeleon**, a map from ML-KEM keys/ciphertexts to uniform strings. This preempts any primitive-level “we make PQ material look like noise” claim.
- **Hybrid Obfuscated Key Exchange and KEMs** (Günther, Rosenberg, Stebila, Veitch, CRYPTO 2025; ePrint 2025/408) `[R]` — supplies a hybrid OKEM combiner with hybrid ciphertext-obfuscation guarantees and a hybrid obfuscated protocol. If p2party ever needs clear/outer KEM bytes to look random before DTLS, this—not an ad-hoc encoding—is the relevant construction.

**Research — ratchet-security models (definitional baseline; target C4's "we are formalizably a Double Ratchet" and C3's PQ-soundness).**

- **The Double Ratchet: Security Notions, Proofs, and Modularization** (Alwen, Coretti, Dodis, EUROCRYPT 2019) `[R]` — the KEM-abstraction (CKA) antecedent; establishes the academic PQ-ratchet line focuses on the security-proof side, never transport-level concealment. It is the right model for the internal sparse epoch state machine as that core is integrated. The current p2party bootstrap and healing construction are **not** exact ACD-CKA/X-Wing instantiations and cannot inherit that theorem by citation; the paper must prove the actual composition or state an engineered assumption.
- **A Formal Security Analysis of the Signal Messaging Protocol** (Cohn-Gordon, Cremers, Dowling, Garratt, Stebila, IEEE **EuroS&P 2017**; extended **Journal of Cryptology 33(4), 2020**) `[R]` — the as-implemented security model. **Attribute precisely as EuroS&P / J. Cryptology — NOT Oakland IEEE S&P and NOT EUROCRYPT/CRYPTO.** This is bedrock for the vocabulary and proof obligations (FS, PCS, immediate decryption) that a future p2party proof should reuse; it does not make p2party's current PQ composition sound by citation and it cannot preempt a systems contribution.
- **Towards Bidirectional Ratcheted Key Exchange** (Poettering & Rösler, CRYPTO 2018) `[R]` and **Efficient Ratcheting: Almost-Optimal Guarantees for Secure Messaging** (Jost, Maurer, Mularczyk, EUROCRYPT 2019) `[R]` — stronger ratchet-security-model and efficiency/PCS comparison points, one abstraction layer above any implementation.

**In-the-wild / standards (contrast cases).**

- **SPQR** (Signal blog + `signalapp/SparsePostQuantumRatchet`, 2025) `[I]` — shreds ML-KEM-768 EK (1184 B → ~37 chunks) and CT (1088 B → ~34 chunks) into **32-byte erasure-coded** chunks (deliberately matched to the legacy ECDH ratchet's 32-byte-per-message overhead so the wire format doesn't change size) piggybacked on ordinary messages, precisely because the existing transport can't carry >1 KB cheaply and a size spike would enable a downgrade/censorship attack. The **mirror image** of C3. Does **not** hide chunks in decoys. (Signal's blog says only "erasure codes" — do **not** attribute "Reed–Solomon"; the exact code family is in the EUROCRYPT 2025 paper body and is unverified from primary text — see §7 Gaps.)
- **iMessage PQ3** (Apple, 2024) `[I]` — the opposite tradeoff: sends the full ~2 KB ciphertext directly and throttles rekey frequency (~every 50 messages / ≥ every 7 days, because a PQ-KEM rekey "increases message size by more than 2 kilobytes"); pads message length separately via Padmé. Never disguises the rekey. Real deployed evidence a big PQ ct is _not_ free on a normal transport. (Deployment in Apple's official write-up and F. Jacobs, _Designing iMessage PQ3_, Real World Crypto 2024 invited talk; formally analyzed separately by Linker et al., USENIX Sec 2025.) Does not preempt C3.
- **PQShield "Diving into Signal's new PQ protocol"** (2025) `[I/blog]` — secondary source that states **"42-byte chunks"**; this figure is **untrustworthy** and must not be cited over the source-verified 32-byte constant.
- **FIPS 203 (ML-KEM)** (NIST, 2024) `[S]` — authoritative CT sizes 768/1088/1568 B — grounds the "fits in one 64 KiB chunk" arithmetic.
- **X-Wing: The Hybrid KEM** (Barbosa, Connolly, Duarte, Kaiser, Schwabe, Varner, Westerbaan) `[R/draft]` — published in **IACR Communications in Cryptology 2024** (a **journal**, ePrint 2024/039), with companion active individual Internet-Draft 10. Hybrid X25519+ML-KEM-768 ciphertext is a fixed **1,120 B ≈ 1.71% of a historical 64-KiB frame**, with no fragmentation logic. The I-D is not an RFC, and the KEM is neither interactive nor authenticated. It supports only the transport-size comparison; it does not name, standardize, or prove p2party's handshake.
- **Kemeleon / OKEM** (CCS 2024; RWC 2025 presentation) `[R]` — establishes that raw ML-KEM public keys and ciphertexts are not computationally indistinguishable from uniform random bytes and supplies an encoding that is. **Layering correction:** that fact is a blocker only if the declared observer sees the raw KEM encoding. A passive direct/TURN observer of p2party sees an encrypted WebRTC/DTLS record; if the KEM control stays inside the authenticated fixed-size application cell and DTLS envelope, Kemeleon's raw-byte distinguisher has no input. Packet traces must confirm this. Kemeleon remains mandatory prior art and becomes implementation-required if KEM material is ever exposed outside that envelope.

### E. In-the-wild browser / P2P E2E ratchet & transfer implementations (targets C4)

- **Zerion 2.x / 3.0 source** (Android/Tor, 2026) `[I]` — the closest applied C3 comparator. Tagged 2.0.7 is downloadable and its release line carries a per-message ML-KEM-768 hybrid ratchet. Public commit `1f9d00fce039fd99c2cd90e14da3a213f6ffa80a` adds the v3 ZWF/ZPP transport: exact **4096-byte** frames, one real-or-cover frame per roughly **750 ms ±1/3 jitter**, with fresh ML-KEM-768 material per frame. The v3 cover transport is currently source, not a tagged release. It differs materially: Android over persistent Tor streams, per-frame KEM, and no working independent classical DH ratchet in the cited v3 whitepaper; p2party is browser WebRTC, one ephemeral DataChannel per logical message, and proposes sparse PQ advances substituted into an optional room schedule. Nevertheless, Zerion invalidates “no deployed/public implementation” and is the primary head-to-head systems baseline.
- **p2party.com** `[I, current deployment boundary]` — a publicly tryable browser-native WebRTC mesh today. The frontend repository now installs the rebuilt 0.12 ML-KEM/ratchet package, but that exact artifact has not yet passed the fresh full-WebRTC T5 gate or production deployment verification; the authenticated room-wide cover scheduler is unimplemented. Do not count the live site as deployment evidence for C3.
- **Matrix — Olm / Megolm / vodozemac / matrix-rust-sdk-crypto-wasm / matrix-js-sdk** (2016–2025) `[I]` — **production Double Ratchet in the browser** (Element Web) via WASM, with **IndexedDB** session persistence. Does _not_ use non-extractable WebCrypto keys (keys live in WASM linear memory / optionally-encrypted IndexedDB blob) and requires a homeserver account (not permissionless URL).
- **Wire — Proteus / @wireapp/core** (2016–2024) `[I]` — genuine Double Ratchet in-browser (libsodium WASM), IndexedDB sessions. No non-extractable-WebCrypto storage; requires a Wire backend.
- **Wire — core-crypto / OpenMLS** (2022–2025) `[I]` — MLS (RFC 9420) compiled to WASM with "encrypted persistent storage"; its own docs admit the WASM encryption-at-rest "isn't validated nor audited."
- **Wrongthink** (FlintSH, 2023–2024) `[I]` — **the single strongest C4 threat**: a browser app advertised literally as "peer-to-peer … end-to-end encryption (Double Ratchet over DTLS (WebRTC))", PeerJS signaling + in-house TweetNaCl ratchet. Niche/unaudited, PeerJS-ID (not a shared-URL mesh), no documented IndexedDB/non-extractable-key story, zero traffic-analysis defense.
- **2key-ratchet** (PeculiarVentures) `[I]` — Signal Double Ratchet + X3DH ported to **WebCrypto** in TypeScript, predating protocol-v3. Two-party, not WebRTC/mesh; no confirmed non-extractable-key/IndexedDB use; substitutes P-256 for X25519. Now archived, points to `pqc-ratchet`.
- **pqc-ratchet** (PeculiarVentures) `[I]` — a **PQ** Double Ratchet (ML-KEM-768+X25519, ML-DSA-65) browser-compatible via WebCrypto + `@noble/post-quantum`. Undercuts a naive "first PQ ratchet in a browser" framing. No transport/chunking, IndexedDB, mesh, or PAKE. "Not production-ready."
- **positive-intentions "P2P Signal Protocol"** (2023–2026) `[I]` — Signal Double Ratchet ported to P2P WebRTC in-browser, live X3DH handshake, server-less. IndexedDB persistence listed as _future_ work; no non-extractable-key discussion; strictly two-party, online-only; no mesh/chunking.
- **ts-mls** (Luka Jacobowitz) `[I]` — TypeScript MLS (RFC 9420) with PQ/hybrid ciphersuites (ML-KEM, X-Wing), WebCrypto-capable. MLS not Double Ratchet; no documented IndexedDB/non-extractable-key persistence.
- **Magic Wormhole** (Warner; CLI + `wormhole.page` WASM) `[I]` — SPAKE2-from-codeword → NaCl secretbox file transfer. No ratchet, no chunk/decoy transport, no DTLS-fingerprint/identity binding. Establishes PAKE-over-P2P-transfer as commodity.
- **PairDrop / Snapdrop** (schlagmichdoch/RobinLinus) `[I]` and **FilePizza** (kern) `[I]` — shared-URL → WebRTC transfer whose confidentiality "is mandatory … completely done by the browser itself" (bare DTLS). **Negative precedent**: the deployed browser-transfer niche does _nothing_ at the C1/C2/C3/C5 level — that space is open.
- **js-libp2p WebRTC** (Protocol Labs) `[I]` — real permissionless browser-to-browser WebRTC mesh, PeerId-authenticated, DTLS + Noise AKE. Noise is a per-session AKE, not a persistent ratchet; no PAKE/PIN, no chunk defense.

### F. Browser at-rest key protection & WebCrypto (targets C4)

- **Web Cryptography API** (W3C Rec 2017 / L2 draft 2025) `[S]` — defines the `CryptoKey` `extractable` slot; the **substrate** C4 builds on, not competing prior art.
- **Security Analysis of the W3C Web Cryptography API** (Cairns, Halpin, Steel, SSR 2016) `[R]` — formal (AVISPA/SAT) analysis; a key-wrapping attack on secret material was found and "remains open." The citation for honestly caveating non-extractability as _not_ HSM-equivalent (an XSS attacker can't exfiltrate the key but _can invoke it_ while injected).
- **WebCrypto API: Prevalence and Possible Developer Mistakes** (ACM DL, 2022; authorship unconfirmed) `[R]` — empirical study documenting real-world key-extractability mistakes; shows correct non-extractable usage is _not_ the default, i.e. nontrivial engineering.
- **Storing Cryptographic Keys in Persistent Browser Storage** (Corella, Pomcor, 2017) `[R]` — **threatens the C4 mechanism narrowly**: explicitly proposes generating a non-extractable WebCrypto keypair and persisting the `CryptoKey` in IndexedDB as an at-rest scheme, discussing XSS/malware/physical-capture. Establishes "non-extractable CryptoKey persisted in IndexedDB" as a **known general pattern since 2017**.
- **secure-webstore** (AKASHAorg) `[I]` — encrypted IndexedDB via a passphrase-derived key held in JS memory (_not_ a non-extractable CryptoKey). Negative data point: "secure browser storage" libraries commonly do _not_ adopt the non-extractable pattern, so p2party's choice is deliberate, not boilerplate.
- **SoK: Secure Messaging** (Unger et al., IEEE S&P 2015) `[R]` — the expected reviewer baseline that **explicitly scopes out endpoint compromise** ("we assume that the endpoints … are secure"; "malware and hardware attacks are out of scope"). C4's contribution — non-extractable WebCrypto keys defeating JS-level key exfiltration after an origin compromise — sits _precisely in the gap this canonical SoK declares out of scope_. Frame C4 as a deliberate relaxation of that endpoint-security assumption. (Also the field taxonomy relevant to C1/C2/C5; see §G.)

### G. PAKE & channel binding to transport certs (targets C5)

**Standards / drafts.**

- **CPace** (draft-irtf-cfrg-cpace-21; Haase, Labrique) `[S]` — defines CI (channel identifier) and ADa/ADb associated-data fields to bind the PAKE to "networking addresses … or party identity strings," but leaves the integration entirely to the application and never discusses a TLS/DTLS certificate or fingerprint. Anticipates C5's move in the abstract; does not specify it.
- **RFC 9266 (TLS 1.3 channel bindings)** `[S]`, **RFC 5056 (channel bindings)** `[S]` — the generic "bind an outer auth protocol to the underlying secure channel" primitives (SASL/GSS-API/EAP over TLS). Never applied to DTLS/WebRTC, never fused with a PAKE, never fold in a separate identity key. (See §7 Gaps: the `tls-exporter`/`tls-unique` / RFC 5929 lineage is the likely nearest prior art to search next.)
- **RFC 8122 (SDP DTLS fingerprint)** `[S]` — the exact fingerprint-binding primitive p2party's PAKE input consumes; but provides **no defense when signaling itself is adversarial** (fingerprint is simply substituted). This is the vulnerability C5 closes, not a solution.
- **RFC 8827 (WebRTC Security Architecture / IdP)** `[S]` — **threatens C5's framing, not its mechanism**: WebRTC already binds a peer's asserted identity to the DTLS fingerprint to defeat a malicious signaling server ("can strip … but cannot forge" assertions). But it requires a **trusted federated Identity Provider**, and a malicious signaling server _can still strip_ the assertion. The paper must cite this and argue the PAKE+PIN route removes the IdP trust anchor entirely.
- **RFC 6189 (ZRTP)** `[S]` — the classic human-verified-SAS + key-continuity/TOFU MITM defense. Runs its own DH in the SRTP _media_ path, uses a spoken SAS (not a PAKE), never touches a DTLS certificate fingerprint or a persistent identity key.
- **RFC 9807 (OPAQUE)** `[S]` — asymmetric client-server aPAKE; different trust model than balanced peer-to-peer CPace; no transport/identity channel binding. Included for PAKE-landscape completeness.

**Research / implementations.**

- **Security Analysis of CPace** (Abdalla, Haase, Hesse, ASIACRYPT 2021, pp. 711–741) `[R]` — the **primitive's security pedigree**: a UC-secure-under-adaptive-corruptions proof of the exact **balanced** PAKE C5 builds on (both sides hold the PIN; no server-side verifier to steal; MapToPoint not idealized). But it takes a **generic, opaque channel/session identifier** input, treated as environment-supplied auxiliary data — the proof never specifies or evaluates a real-world instantiation of that slot. C5's novelty is precisely _what_ fills it. Cite as the pedigree that justifies using CPace rather than rolling one's own.
- **Universally Composable Relaxed Password Authenticated Key Exchange** (Abdalla, Barbosa, Bradley, Jarecki, Katz, Xu, CRYPTO 2020) `[R]` — the lazy-extraction PAKE UC toolkit CPace's own analysis builds on; background for the security argument.
- **OPAQUE: An Asymmetric PAKE Secure Against Pre-Computation** (Jarecki, Krawczyk, Xu, EUROCRYPT 2018) `[R]` — the aPAKE counterpoint at the paper level (complementing RFC 9807); motivates why p2party picks a _balanced_ PAKE.
- **Triple Handshakes and Cookie Cutters: Breaking and Fixing Authentication over TLS** (Bhargavan, Delignat-Lavaud, Fournet, Pironti, Strub, IEEE S&P 2014) `[R]` — establishes that **binding higher-layer auth to the transport session/certificate** is a known, respected technique for closing cross-protocol MITM gaps — so C5's _general principle_ is established, not invented here. But it is a TLS attack/fix paper, not a PAKE-input-binding construction.
- **SoK: Secure Messaging** (Unger et al., IEEE S&P 2015) `[R]` — the canonical taxonomy of trust-establishment (PKI, key directories, TOFU, OOB/SAS, password-based). Situates C5's PAKE-based trust in the known design space; does not itself bind a PAKE to WebRTC fingerprints + identity key.
- **PakeMail** (Vazquez Sandoval, Atashpendar, Lenzini, Ryan, arXiv 2021) `[R]` — closest academic analog in spirit: PAKE to bootstrap authentication in a decentralized (no-PKI, no-TTP) messaging setting from a low-entropy secret. But targets email/XMPP identity, never binds a DTLS fingerprint, no data-channel or Ed25519-device component.
- **How to kickstart Secure Message Transfer with SAS and OOB** (Beskorovajnov & Müller-Quade, ePrint 2025/1598) `[R]` — very recent; critiques "PAKE one-code" designs (Magic-Wormhole-style) and argues SAS+OOB composed with KEM/DEM is preferable, decoupling rendezvous from authentication. Observes that consumer/WebRTC-style channels commonly lack a user-verifiable binding to the transport — exactly C5's gap — but its own fix is SAS/OOB, not a PAKE bound to DTLS fingerprints. Strong motivating/contrast citation.
- **Cryptographic Binding Should Not Be Optional (FIDO UAF)** (Golaszewski et al., SSR 2025) `[R]` — Dolev-Yao proof that optional/incomplete channel binding enables cross-session relay. General motivation for why C5's binding must be mandatory and cryptographically enforced; scoped to FIDO UAF, not PAKE/WebRTC.
- **Reclaiming our Passwords: Protecting E2EE from a Malicious Zoom Server** (Cremers, Ronen, Zhao, Real World Crypto 2024) `[R]` — "PAKE against a malicious coordination server" is an active real-world theme; not DTLS-fingerprint binding, but the closest deployed-motivation neighbor for C5's trust model.
- **webwormhole** (saljam, 2019–2024) `[I]` — **threatens C5**: already runs **CPace** (the same PAKE) over the WebRTC exchange and **binds both peers' DTLS certificate fingerprints** into the PAKE-protected session descriptions, in-browser. Preempts "CPace binding DTLS fingerprints" as a novel mechanism. Does **not** bind long-term Ed25519 identity keys and has **no persistent ratchet** (one-shot ephemeral PAKE per transfer).
- **Magic-Wormhole client-to-client protocol** (Warner et al.) `[I]` — balanced SPAKE2 with idA/idB identity strings folded into key-derivation + an optional manual "verifier." Shows PAKE-with-bound-identity-strings is established, but relays over a mailbox server via plain TCP — no certificate fingerprint to bind.

### H. Blind meeting points, private presence, and private signaling (targets C6)

- **DP5** (Borisov, Danezis, Goldberg, PoPETs 2015) `[R]` — the closest conceptual ancestor: epoch pseudonyms + PIR hide friend presence and allow authenticated auxiliary data explicitly intended to bootstrap direct peer-to-peer communication. It assumes a pairwise secret already exists for each friendship. That preempts “private presence can bootstrap P2P,” but not a dynamic capability room whose participants have not pre-registered pairwise relationships.
- **Private Signaling** (Madathil et al., USENIX Security 2022 Distinguished Paper) `[R]` — formally defines private bulletin-board notification and gives constant sender/recipient work using either one TEE server or two noncolluding garbled-circuit servers. It preempts private signaling as a new primitive; p2party's question is how to compose such a facility with dynamic n-party discovery and short-lived WebRTC signaling records.
- **Signal Private Groups** (Chase et al., CCS 2020/full version) `[R/I]` — hides the encrypted roster and authenticates membership with anonymous credentials, proving that a service need not know a group's membership list. It deliberately does not hide access patterns. The 2025 _No Safety in Numbers_ recipient/receipt timing attack is an important empirical warning: encrypted group state without access-pattern cover does not give relationship privacy.
- **Talek** (Cheng et al., ACSAC 2020) `[R]` — provides access-sequence indistinguishability for fixed-rate real/fake reads and writes under a three-server anytrust model; its prototype reports 9,433 messages/s for 32,000 active users at 1.7-second latency. It is a plausible simple substrate/comparator, not the current efficiency frontier. The mismatch is load-bearing: Talek exposes a **single-writer, multi-reader log**, while p2party needs a dynamic multi-writer presence table with private capacity allocation, collision handling, expiry, and WebRTC handoff.
- **Myco** (Kaviani et al., IEEE S&P 2025) `[R]` — the missing SOTA correction. It replaces quadratic PIR with an oblivious data structure in an asymmetric two-server distributed-trust design, achieves `O(N log² N)`, and reports up to 302×/2,219× throughput gains over multi-/single-server PIR baselines. A p2party L2 prototype may still choose Talek for simplicity, but the paper must compare that choice against Myco rather than call Talek strongest.
- **Peer2PIR** (Mazmudar, Veitch, Akhavan Mahdavi, IEEE S&P 2025) `[R]` — integrates PIR into real IPFS for private peer routing, provider advertisements, and content retrieval. It is not an n-party presence/SDP rendezvous protocol, but it directly preempts generic claims that private discovery in a P2P network is new.
- **2PPS** (Gaballah et al., 2021 preprint) `[R]` — DPF private writes + PIR private reads provide publisher and subscriber privacy if at least one replica is honest, with a 100,000-client/5-second prototype result. It reinforces both the anytrust direction and the cost: every client participates in each round.
- **Pung** (Angel, Setty, OSDI 2016) and **DPIR** (Tovey, Weiss, Gilad, 2024 preprint) `[R]` — demonstrate the honest single-server / fully untrusted-server alternative. Pung pays heavy CPIR cost; DPIR improves scaling by offloading validated work to clients. These are required baselines for the claim that anytrust is a pragmatic rather than cryptographically necessary choice.
- **Riposte** and **Express** `[R]` — private-write systems are relevant building blocks, but incomplete rendezvous solutions by themselves. Express explicitly focuses on anonymous message delivery while leaking which mailbox is read; Riposte is write-oriented and latency tolerant. A blind write address does not privatize discovery and reads.
- **PingPong** (2025 preprint) `[R]` — recent “notify before retrieval” metadata-private messaging with uniform traffic; useful SOTA context, but enclave-dependent and evaluated with a 32-server deployment assumption. Treat it as a preprint, not a deployed baseline.
- **OPRF (RFC 9497), Privacy Pass (RFCs 9576–9578), and OHTTP (RFC 9458)** `[S]` — useful supporting mechanisms with sharply different jobs. An OPRF hides the input while evaluating a PRF, but submitting one shared output gives the broker an equality handle and is only L1. Privacy Pass can supply unlinkable quota credentials; it is not an inbox. OHTTP splits client IP from request content under noncollusion; it neither hides that several clients touch the same mailbox nor survives relay/gateway collusion.
- **Tor onion-service rendezvous** `[I/S]` — a deployed blind meeting point for one location-hidden service endpoint, not server-blind n-party room membership. Direct WebRTC also sits outside Tor after bootstrap unless the data plane is deliberately relayed.

**SOTA conclusion:** there is no free cryptographic shortcut to asynchronous, low-latency relationship hiding; current systems move cost among client bandwidth, server work/storage, TEEs, and noncollusion assumptions. Myco materially improves the two-server efficiency frontier, while Peer2PIR shows that private P2P queries are already practical enough to evaluate in a large real protocol. The p2party working point remains an **unimplemented** short-TTL anytrust rendezvous adaptation: DPF/Riposte-style private point writes plus batched IT-PIR reads for the dynamic multi-writer presence board, optional Talek-like directional single-writer logs only after pairwise discovery, and Myco as the efficiency comparator. It uses a 256-bit fragment capability, rotating presence/inbox identifiers, fixed-size encrypted SDP/ICE records, and service-wide real-or-dummy accesses. L2a hides the application graph; L2b additionally needs independent ingress/TURN or an anonymity network because source IP, timing, direct WebRTC endpoints, and a colluding TURN operator remain visible.

### I. Private BitTorrent and protocol-extension prior art (targets C7)

The proposed work is an actual BitTorrent discovery/wire extension, not “use BitTorrent's DHT” and not merely “run BitTorrent through p2party.”

- **BEP 3** `[S]` exposes the `info_hash`, peer ID, bitfield/HAVE state, piece indices, requests, and cancellations. Trackers receive a common swarm key plus peer endpoints. The standard handshake is deliberately distinguishable and carries the `info_hash` before any BEP 10 extension message can run.
- **BEP 5 / BEP 42 / BEP 44** `[S]` do not create a blind meeting point. Mainline DHT lookups expose a common target and source endpoint; BEP 42 raises the cost of targeted node-ID placement/snooping but does not hide swarm membership; BEP 44 values are not private and cannot safely be assumed to exceed 1,000 encoded bytes—smaller than one ML-KEM-768 public key.
- **BEP 10 / BEP 11 / BEP 55** `[S]` are too late or too revealing for strong mode. BEP 10 negotiates only after the leaking standard handshake; PEX exports compact live peer endpoints; hole punching gives a rendezvous peer both endpoint addresses. These features must be replaced or disabled, not wrapped in optimistic fallback.
- **BEP 27** `[S]` is access control (“private torrent”), not metadata privacy: it disables DHT/PEX and requires the tracker, which still sees the swarm. **BEP 37** `[S]` is valuable fail-closed proxy hygiene and explicitly documents cross-swarm peer-ID/port/LSD leaks, but does not hide logical swarm membership from the tracker/proxy.
- **I2P BitTorrent + ECIES-X25519-AEAD-Ratchet** `[I/S]` is the decisive PQ/ratchet adjacency. I2P specifies BitTorrent trackers, DHT, and PEX over I2P; its end-to-end ECIES protocol incorporates Signal's Double Ratchet. Proposal 169 reports hybrid ML-KEM ratchet implementations complete in Java I2P and i2pd and a November 2025 release; the April 2026 hybrid spec still correctly describes wider rollout as implementation-dependent/in progress. Its initiator-ML-KEM-public-key/responder-ciphertext Noise pattern is also the same high-level bootstrap direction p2party uses. Therefore “first BitTorrent + Double Ratchet + ML-KEM” is false.
- **Peer2PIR** (IEEE S&P 2025) `[R]` is the decisive private-P2P-query adjacency: private peer routing, provider advertisements, and content retrieval for IPFS with measured overhead. IPFS is not BitTorrent and Peer2PIR does not provide room capabilities, a ratchet, fixed-rate peer-wire cover, or incentives, but “PIR-backed private discovery/retrieval in a P2P content network” is already done.
- **OneSwarm** (SIGCOMM 2010) `[R/I]` is the strongest historical application-layer comparator: capability-controlled sharing, disposable addresses, multi-hop privacy-preserving lookup/transfer, and legacy BitTorrent content bridging. **Tribler** `[I]` provides deployed Tor-like onion routing for torrent downloads and experimental hidden seeding. **Aqua** (SIGCOMM 2013), the BitTorrent Anonymity Marketplace, and **Octopus** anonymous DHT lookup explore the anonymity/performance/incentive/discovery frontier. BitTorrent MSE/PE obfuscates protocol structure but is fingerprintable and provides no relationship cover.

**Remaining opening:** a fail-closed private swarm can use D5 rendezvous, authenticate an X25519 + exact swarm-policy-selected ML-KEM-512/768/1024 outer p2party session with a swarm capability, and carry conventional HAVE/request/piece/cancel semantics in fixed authenticated cells under a swarm-wide rate profile. It must preserve sparse bounded-neighbor topology, piece/Merkle verification, choking, rarest-first, resume, and cancellation. Dummy bytes never earn reciprocity credit. Content semantics can remain compatible; strong-private discovery and wire interoperability with legacy peers cannot. The academically interesting result would be the measured **private logical swarm discovery + fixed-cell cover + incentive/throughput** trade-off, not the primitive bundle.

**Carrier and framing boundary:** current p2party bootstrap is hidden by WebRTC/DTLS. A raw-TCP BitTorrent extension would expose a distinctive 2,465-byte HELLO and raw ML-KEM fields, so strong P2BT must either use WebRTC/DTLS, bind `createSession()` to a TLS 1.3/QUIC exporter, or adopt a published OKE/Kemeleon construction. Encrypting after a fingerprintable custom bootstrap supports confidentiality and logical-swarm access control, not a traffic-camouflage claim. Covered BitTorrent should also multiplex logical transfer IDs over a long-lived neighbor connection rather than copy chat's per-message physical DataChannel: stream creation, closure, and request-level churn are themselves demand signals.

**Rate arithmetic:** the present frame admits at most 61,919 useful bytes. One cell/10 s is approximately 49.5 kbit/s per direction and 48.2 hours/GiB from one source; one cell/60 s is approximately 8.26 kbit/s and 289 hours/GiB. Therefore cadence alone is not a bulk design. The independent variable is a predeclared swarm-wide cell-rate profile (possibly predefined bulk epochs), and profile changes may not follow demand. ML-KEM material has zero _marginal observer-visible cells/bytes_ only when it consumes padding in an already-scheduled cell; it still costs CPU and useful capacity, and it displaces piece data when the link is saturated.

## 3. Per-claim novelty assessment

Verdict scale: **NOVEL** / **NOVEL-ADJACENT** (incremental over known work) / **ALREADY-DONE** (someone shipped or published it). After the 2026-07-23 adjudication, **none of C1–C7 is established as primitive-level NOVEL**. C3+C6 form the strongest main-paper direction, but only as an architecture-specific, experimentally unproven systems claim; C7 is a secondary direction until implemented.

### C1 — Fixed-cell active-transfer shaping over browser WebRTC/DTLS

**Closest prior art:** Sphinx (S&P 2009, uniform packet defeats a traffic observer, traces to Chaum 1981); Tamaraw (CCS 2014, constant-rate padded-to-uniform — the sharpest single threat); PURBs (PoPETs 2019, uniform single blob); SoK: Metadata-Protecting Communication Systems (PoPETs 2024, the taxonomy); Maybenot (WPES 2023) / DeTorrent (PoPETs 2024) (padding frameworks / padding-only defenses are a crowded line); Tarzan (P2P indistinguishable cover traffic, link-level); Loopix (cover traffic vs. traffic analysis, sender-pays); Slitheen / decoy routing (only the keyholder extracts real bytes from what looks like cover); obfs4 & BitTorrent MSE (in-the-wild padded/random-looking transports); Karaoke/Vuvuzela (fixed-size + noise + shuffle, server mixnet); Strong Anonymity for Mesh Messaging (P2P + metadata privacy, whole-message granularity). Evaluation baselines to beat: Deep Fingerprinting (CCS 2018) and Rimmer (NDSS 2018); attack to survive: the Last Hop Attack class (PoPETs 2025).

**How p2party differs:** This pass did not find a prior system combining the exact current bundle: fixed 65,490-byte application chunk frames **+** real data at a random in-chunk offset **+** per-chunk random padding **+** pure-noise decoy chunks **+** shuffled/flooded multi-peer delivery **+** Merkle-authenticated resumable reassembly **+** exact 65-byte tagged true-or-random receipts, in a browser WebRTC full mesh. That is an implementation-bundle observation, not a proof of trace privacy or priority. RFCs 8831/8832 make the limitation concrete: SCTP/DTLS fragmentation, DCEP OPEN/ACK, stream reset, retransmission, loss, and congestion can remain classifiable even when application buffers are equal. The _category_ ("uniform / fixed-size, content-independent packaging reduces size/format leakage") is decades old (Sphinx→Chaum 1981) and **must not be claimed**.

**Verdict: NOVEL-ADJACENT.** This survey found no publication or shipped system with the exact bundle and browser/WebRTC realization, but that negative search is not a priority proof and every ingredient has a named ancestor. Claim the _specific mechanism bundle + browser transport realization + DTLS-mesh threat model + the C3 tie-in_, explicitly disclaiming both the underlying "uniformity as defense" principle (Sphinx/Tamaraw/PURBs) and any current idle-time/timing-cover property. Benchmark against the DL attacks, not just count/size heuristics.

### C2 — "Offensive cryptography": economic/adversarial cost-imposition on a passive metadata observer

**Closest prior art:** Dwork–Naor "Pricing via Processing" (CRYPTO 1992, the origin of cost-asymmetric defense) and Client Puzzles (NDSS 1999) for the "impose asymmetric cost on an adversary" abstraction; Chaffing-and-winnowing (Rivest 1998) for the "attacker must sift indistinguishable chaff" mechanism; Pung (_fully untrusted infrastructure_, OSDI 2016) for the only system that makes untrusted infrastructure do provably expensive work (but on a _server's CPU_, not a passive eavesdropper); EMC2 (WPES 2024) for the closest "must observe ALL channels" surveillance-cost argument; TEEMS (PoPETs 2025) as the TEE contrast; Dissent/Herbivore for DC-net "everyone transmits" pedigree (which explicitly minimizes waste); On the Economics of Anonymity for the term "economics" (participant incentives, a different sense); Traffic Morphing as the explicit _opposite_ philosophy; the HNDL-feasibility paper as the direct critique.

**How p2party differs:** p2party targets a **passive direct-path network observer or TURN relay carrying the already-established association** and frames the defense as forcing that observer to buffer/store/analyze mostly-junk to find the few real bits — a real-time correlation/selection burden. The signaling service is a separate control-plane observer and does not automatically see DataChannel payload traffic. Dwork–Naor and Client Puzzles **invert every concrete parameter**: their cost falls on the message _sender_ via a _solved CPU puzzle_ at the application layer; C2's cost falls on an _involuntary passive observer_, is _storage + downstream cryptanalytic effort_ forced by decoy volume, and no one solves a puzzle. Rivest's argument is cryptographic indistinguishability, not resource economics; the anonymity literature's "economics" is participation game theory; EMC2 is cross-channel secret-sharing ("observe all channels"), not intra-mesh decoy volume ("store all chunks").

**Verdict: NOVEL-ADJACENT — and the weakest standalone claim.** The economic-defense _lineage_ is old (Dwork–Naor 1992 onward) and the chaff _mechanism_ is Rivest 1998 / the anonymity cover-traffic line; only the _direction of the asymmetry_ (burden an involuntary observer to protect metadata) and the _decoy-volume storage mechanism_ are fresh, and this is rhetorical/positioning more than a new technique. Reviewers will cite Dwork–Naor, Rivest, and the now-verified HNDL feasibility preprint. Present C2 as a framing/positioning of C1, own the Dwork–Naor lineage explicitly, and distinguish "waste analysis effort _now_" from "inflate storage for _later_."

### C3 — Sparse authenticated PQ advances substituted into a room-wide fixed-cell WebRTC schedule

**Closest prior art:** Triple Ratchet (EUROCRYPT 2025) + SPQR (2025) + iMessage PQ3 (RWC 2024 / USENIX Sec 2025) + Auerbach et al. “How to Compare Bandwidth Constrained…” (USENIX Sec 2025) + Alwen–Coretti–Dodis (EUROCRYPT 2019); fixed-packet neighbors Post Quantum Sphinx (2023) and Outfox (2024/2025); obfuscated-KEM work OKE/Kemeleon (CCS 2024) and Hybrid OKE (CRYPTO 2025); browser `pqc-ratchet`; and, most importantly, the applied Zerion 3.0 source design (fixed 4096-byte constant-rate frames + per-frame ML-KEM over Tor).

**What is already done:** A large PQ ciphertext fitting inside a larger fixed frame is arithmetic, not a cryptographic invention. Fixed-size traffic shaping is old; fixed PQ/KEM packet formats exist; Zerion now publicly combines constant-rate fixed frames with in-frame ML-KEM in a messenger. OKE/Kemeleon already handles the stronger primitive-level problem of making exposed KEM transcripts look random. Signal and Apple already deploy hybrid PQ ratchets.

**The narrower p2party hypothesis:** p2party can compare a different point in the design space: an optional, authenticated **room-wide** schedule over WebRTC; **sparse** full-size ML-KEM CKA advances that replace a dummy cell only when the standard Triple-Ratchet state machine needs one; and an ephemeral DataChannel per logical message for cancel/progress/backpressure/retry/teardown isolation. `n>2` already ships: every room member becomes a peer and the WebRTC data plane is a full pairwise mesh; only signaling/discovery is centralized and star-shaped. Relative to a schedule that would send the cell anyway, a PQ advance can add zero marginal observer-visible application frames/bytes and avoid Zerion's permanent per-frame KEM compute/header tax. That does not make the room cheap: the dummy schedule scales across `n(n−1)/2` edges.

**Verdict: NOVEL-ADJACENT and paper-worthy only if measured.** Do not claim “first,” “only,” “unpreempted,” “PQ ciphertexts are free,” or a new Triple Ratchet. The publishable contribution would be the implemented browser/WebRTC architecture and an empirical result showing a useful security/performance point relative to SPQR/PQ3/Zerion. Four qualifications are load-bearing:

1. Say **zero marginal scheduled application frames/bytes**, never zero absolute cost. Report idle bandwidth, TURN egress, CPU/battery, channel churn, mesh scaling, and overflow.
2. Immediate/no-cover is the product default. Only rooms that authenticate and actually maintain a demand-independent schedule receive the cover claim.
3. Raw ML-KEM non-uniformity is not visible to the declared pre-DTLS/TURN observer when the record stays inside application AEAD + DTLS. Kemeleon remains cited and becomes necessary if the raw encoding crosses that boundary. Validate the layer assumption with packet captures.
4. ML-KEM is the current hybrid's PQ-confidentiality/HNDL component, but the exact multi-input bootstrap lacks a formal proof; describe the resulting guarantee as an engineered assumption until one exists. Authentication remains Ed25519/X25519-classical unless a separate PQ-authentication migration lands.

Fix the 42→32-byte figure and “erasure codes” (not Reed–Solomon) throughout. C3 remains the paper's anchor, but as a falsifiable systems hypothesis rather than a novelty declaration.

### C4 — Browser-native realization: Signal's Double Ratchet ported to native browser tech (IndexedDB persistence + non-extractable WebCrypto at-rest keys) as a permissionless E2E mesh from a shared URL

**Closest prior art:** Matrix (Olm/vodozemac) and Wire (Proteus) — **production Double Ratchet in the browser via WASM with IndexedDB persistence**; Wrongthink — a browser app literally advertising "Double Ratchet over DTLS (WebRTC)"; 2key-ratchet — Double Ratchet ported to WebCrypto; positive-intentions — Signal DR over P2P WebRTC in-browser; Corella (2017) — the non-extractable-CryptoKey-in-IndexedDB pattern as a _known general technique_; pqc-ratchet — a _PQ_ ratchet already browser-compatible. Definitional/model baseline (one abstraction layer above any implementation, so must-cite but non-preempting): Alwen–Coretti–Dodis (EUROCRYPT 2019), Cohn-Gordon et al. (EuroS&P 2017 / J. Cryptology 2020), Poettering–Rösler (CRYPTO 2018), Jost–Maurer–Mularczyk (EUROCRYPT 2019); SoK: Secure Messaging (S&P 2015), which explicitly scopes endpoint/key-extraction _out_.

**How p2party differs:** The headline "Double Ratchet ported to native browser tech + IndexedDB" is **not** new — it is in production (Matrix, Wire) and in hobby form (Wrongthink, positive-intentions). "DR on WebCrypto" is 2key-ratchet. The non-extractable-key-in-IndexedDB pattern is Corella 2017. The academic-model papers (ACD, Cohn-Gordon) formalize the ratchet as an abstract algorithm with no key-storage substrate, and SoK: Secure Messaging explicitly assumes secure endpoints — so **none of the theory preempts a storage-substrate contribution, and one of them (the SoK) hands us the exact out-of-scope gap C4 fills.** What none of them combine is: a **permissionless mesh from a bare shared URL** (Matrix/Wire need accounts/homeservers; Wrongthink/positive-intentions/2key-ratchet are two-party) **+** non-extractable-WebCrypto keys applied specifically to **live Double-Ratchet chain state at rest** (Matrix/Wire keep keys in WASM memory / encrypted blobs; the browser-ratchet projects have no such story, and none apply Corella's generic pattern to a live ratchet in a WebRTC mesh).

**Verdict: ALREADY-DONE for the headline; NOVEL-ADJACENT only for the specific combination.** Be blunt in the paper: "Double Ratchet in a browser over WebRTC/DTLS" is precedented (cite Wrongthink and positive-intentions to pre-empt the reviewer, and Matrix/Wire for the mature production version). Claim only the _narrow, un-preempted residue_: permissionless-shared-URL mesh **and** non-extractable-WebCrypto sealing of live ratchet chain state persisted in IndexedDB — presented as an engineering integration located exactly in SoK: Secure Messaging's declared-out-of-scope endpoint/key-extraction gap, **not** as a first. (A genuine negative result: no confirmed EUROCRYPT/CRYPTO/S&P/USENIX/CCS/NDSS/PoPETs/RWC paper describes this realization — but the true competition, if any, likely sits in W2SP/blog/engineering write-ups outside the confirmed clusters; see §7.)

### C5 — PIN policy adds draft-21 CPace over the post-DTLS data channel to the mandatory interactive 3DH + ML-KEM bootstrap, binding peer identities, DTLS fingerprints, and room policy

**Closest prior art:** webwormhole — already runs **CPace binding DTLS certificate fingerprints** in-browser (preempts that exact sub-mechanism); Security Analysis of CPace (ASIACRYPT 2021) — the primitive's UC/adaptive-corruption pedigree, with a _generic opaque_ channel-identifier slot C5 fills; Triple Handshakes and Cookie Cutters (S&P 2014) — binding higher-layer auth to the transport session/certificate as an established technique; Universally Composable Relaxed PAKE (CRYPTO 2020) and OPAQUE (EUROCRYPT 2018 / RFC 9807) — the PAKE-landscape pedigree and aPAKE counterpoint; RFC 8827 WebRTC IdP — binds identity to the DTLS fingerprint against a malicious signaling server, but via a trusted IdP; ZRTP — human-SAS MITM defense on the media path; PakeMail — PAKE for decentralized no-TTP trust; Reclaiming our Passwords/Zoom (RWC 2024) — PAKE against a malicious coordination server; CPace draft — anticipates CI/AD binding but leaves it to the app; Magic-Wormhole — PAKE with bound identity strings.

**How p2party differs:** No source found in this pass runs the exact application profile: draft-21 CPace/Ristretto255 over a **post-handshake WebRTC data channel**, with ordered persistent identities, both DTLS fingerprints, room policy, version, and PQ marker bound into the channel input/transcript, then combines only CPace's transcript-derived ISK with the independently mandatory interactive 3DH + ML-KEM root inputs. The ASIACRYPT 2021 proof vets CPace itself but treats the channel identifier as environment-supplied auxiliary data and never proves p2party's larger root composition. Triple Handshakes establishes the _general_ transport-binding principle, but is a TLS attack/fix paper, not a PAKE-input construction. webwormhole uses CPace to protect SDP (including DTLS fingerprints) through signaling but has no persistent identity or ratchet. RFC 8827 achieves fingerprint↔identity binding only via a trusted IdP and only "cannot forge" (still strippable). ZRTP uses SAS, not a PAKE, and never touches a certificate fingerprint.

**Verdict: NOVEL-ADJACENT.** The exact dual-binding + optional-CPace + mandatory-3DH/ML-KEM + ratchet-seeding profile was not found, but each part is prior art and the combined handshake lacks a formal proof. Claim an architecture-specific application profile, not a new PAKE or a security theorem. Cite webwormhole up front for CPace+fingerprint, PQXDH as the higher-assurance hybrid-handshake baseline, and Triple Handshakes/RFC 8827 for channel/trust binding.

### C6 — Dynamic server-blind n-party rendezvous that hands off to a browser WebRTC full mesh

**Closest prior art:** DP5 for private presence plus direct-P2P bootstrap data; Private Signaling for a formal private notification primitive; Signal Private Groups for a server-hidden roster; Talek and 2PPS for anytrust hidden-access group logs/pub-sub; **Myco** for the 2025 polylogarithmic two-server efficiency frontier; Pung and DPIR for the fully untrusted single-server line; **Peer2PIR** for deployed-protocol private routing/provider/content queries in a real P2P network; Alpenhorn for metadata-private dialing; Tor onion services for deployed location-hidden rendezvous.

**How p2party differs:** the proposed object is neither a pre-established friend graph nor a single writer's log. A short-lived room capability admits a changing set of mutually unknown members; clients must privately allocate bounded presence slots, discover all live members, exchange fragmented SDP/ICE through private pairwise inboxes, resolve offerer races, expire stale entries, detect collision/equivocation/partition failures, and then produce exactly `n−1` authenticated WebRTC edges per endpoint. Stable identities stay inside the encrypted handshake. This is an adaptation layer over known PIR/private-write/private-signaling machinery, not a new underlying primitive.

**Verdict: NOVEL-ADJACENT / UNIMPLEMENTED.** The architecture is plausibly paper-worthy only as an evaluated system. Talek is a possible simple substrate, not “the SOTA”; Myco is the efficiency comparator, and Peer2PIR preempts a generic private-P2P-query claim. Required evidence includes broker room-graph inference AUC versus today's explicit roster, join latency/failure as `n` grows, browser/server CPU/storage/egress, access cadence cost, capacity/collision/churn behavior, selective-failure/equivocation tests, and direct-versus-TURN/IP/timing residuals. An opaque or OPRF-derived common room token is not C6 and must not be reported as L2.

### C7 — A fail-closed BitTorrent private-swarm extension using p2party sessions and fixed cells

**Closest prior art:** BitTorrent MSE/PE, BEP 27 private torrents, BEP 37 anonymous proxy mode, OneSwarm, Tribler, Aqua, Octopus, the BitTorrent Anonymity Marketplace, **Peer2PIR's private IPFS routing/provider/content protocols**, and—most importantly—BitTorrent over I2P plus I2P's Double-Ratchet and hybrid ML-KEM specifications/implementations.

**How p2party differs:** the proposed mode hides the **logical swarm control plane** rather than only its network path: D5 private discovery, a capability-authenticated hybrid-PQ outer session before the standard handshake, and fixed authenticated cells carrying HAVE/request/piece/cancel under a demand-independent swarm rate class. It retains a sparse neighbor graph and native BitTorrent piece verification/choking/resume semantics; dummy traffic earns no credit. Strong mode deliberately refuses legacy tracker/DHT/PEX/LSD/plaintext fallback.

**Verdict: ALREADY-DONE for the broad “BitTorrent + anonymous transport + ratchet/PQ” headline; NOVEL-ADJACENT and unproven for the exact private-swarm wire/discovery design.** In the main paper it should begin as a secondary transport-neutral/large-file evaluation of `createSession()`. A separate paper requires a real BEP-shaped implementation and a measured new privacy/throughput/incentive/interoperability point against Peer2PIR, I2P, OneSwarm, Tribler, and Aqua.

## 5. Positioning statement

**The sharpest defensible contribution is an evaluated systems result, not the old C3 slogan:** a server-blind control plane that assembles a browser-native full WebRTC/DTLS room mesh without exposing the room graph or stable identities to the rendezvous service, combined with the implemented mandatory transcript-bound hybrid bootstrap, the implemented-but-not-yet-integrated exact-suite sparse PQ-healing state machine, and an optional handshake-authenticated room schedule that substitutes PQ advances for cells the room would send anyway while preserving per-message cancellation/progress/backpressure through ephemeral DataChannels. The paper asks whether that full architecture provides a useful security/performance point against Myco/Talek/Private Signaling, Signal's sparse amortization, Apple's visible throttled rekeys, and Zerion's constant-rate **per-frame** ML-KEM over persistent Tor streams. It does not assert that p2party's bootstrap is cryptographically superior to the formally analyzed PQXDH baseline.

The paper's honest shape is therefore: **C6 L2 server-blind n-party rendezvous as the control-plane contribution; C1 as the enabling WebRTC data transport; C3 as the sparse-PQ scheduled-cell hypothesis; C5 as supporting peer authentication; C2 as threat-model narrative; and C4 as engineering integration.** Each is prior-art-contested and the value is their measured composition. C7 starts as a secondary transport-neutrality/large-file evaluation on BitTorrent's sparse graph; it deserves a separate paper only after a genuine private-swarm protocol and result exist. The language “free” is allowed only as “zero marginal observer-visible application frames/bytes relative to the configured schedule.” Immediate/no-cover is the product default, so on-edge activity cover applies only to cover-configured rooms; control-plane membership blindness needs its own always-on access-pattern defense and leakage statement. The paper must cite PQXDH, Zerion, Myco, Peer2PIR, DP5/Talek/Private Signaling, and I2P/OneSwarm prominently and state that p2party's dated documents show provenance, not priority.

**Venue class:** applied privacy/systems security — **WPES** or **PoPETs** first. A hardened public deployment with multi-browser direct/TURN captures, classifier evaluation, energy/cost results, and adversarial protocol tests could justify a **USENIX Security / NDSS** submission. This is not a CRYPTO/EUROCRYPT primitive paper. Real World Crypto remains a plausible talk venue for the implementation and the SPQR/PQ3/Zerion design-space comparison.
